4.1 Two room load bearing single-storey building with verandah

The procedure, once, for every building

  1. Read the drawings and list the items of work under sub-heads (§3.6).
  2. Find the wall length by the centre line or long wall – short wall method (Chapter 2). This one length drives most of the estimate.
  3. Take out the quantities item by item into the measurement sheet, working from the bottom of the foundation upward.
  4. Apply the deduction rules of §1.4 for openings.
  5. Analyse the rates (Chapter 6) and abstract the cost (§3.7).

Work bottom-upward and the items come out in a natural order that is hard to forget: excavation → PCC bed → footings → DPC → plinth filling → superstructure masonry → lintels and slab → floor → plaster → doors and windows → finishing.

The three quantities that are always asked

earthwork in foundation = centre-line length × trench width × depth brickwork in superstructure = centre-line length × wall thickness × height − volume of all openings plinth area = length × breadth measured to the OUTER face of the walls

Verandah. A verandah is measured with the building but its walls are usually thinner and it is often open on one or more sides, so its length must be taken separately from the main block. Its plinth area is included in full if it is covered; a projected balcony or open terrace is normally taken at 50%.

Deductions for openings in brickwork. Deduct the full volume of each door and window opening (area of opening × thickness of wall). Do not deduct for lintel bearings up to 0.1 m³, or for openings up to 0.1 m² (§1.4).

Dawadi, worked building estimates; measurement rules per IS 1200.

4.2 Four room frame structured building with reinforcement calculations

A framed building differs from a load-bearing one in what carries the load, and therefore in what is measured:

Load bearingRCC framed
FoundationContinuous strip under every wallIsolated footings under each column, measured by number
Vertical load pathThe wall itselfColumns; the wall is only an infill panel
Wall thicknessThick — 230 to 350 mmThin — often 115 mm partition, 230 mm external
Length usedCentre line of the wallsColumn centres; beams measured clear between columns
Dominant itemMasonryRCC and reinforcement

Order of taking out a framed building

  1. Excavation for each isolated footing — number × length × breadth × depth.
  2. PCC bed, footing pad, and the column pedestal below plinth.
  3. Columns from footing top to slab soffit, then plinth beams, then floor beams.
  4. Slab — area × thickness, deducting large voids only.
  5. Infill brickwork panel by panel, deducting openings.
  6. Reinforcement, from the bar bending schedule.

Reinforcement calculation

This is the part that carries the marks. Steel is measured as the length of each bar in place, converted to weight:

weight per metre w = d² / 162 kg/m ( d in mm ) total weight = (number of bars) × (cutting length of one bar) × w

Cutting length is the clear length plus hooks and bends, less the cover:

straight bar : L = clear span − 2 × cover + 2 × hook one hook = 9d (standard 180° hook) bend allowance : 45° bend adds 1d , 90° bend adds 2d crank (bent-up): add 0.42 D per crank , D = clear depth between the bar centres stirrup : L = perimeter of the stirrup + 2 × hook − bend deductions number of stirrups = (clear span / spacing) + 1

Two rules save marks: always add 1 when counting stirrups or bars from a spacing, and remember that binding wire and rolling margin are not measured separately — they are inside the rate.

Worked pattern for a beam 5 m clear, 30 × 62 cm, with 3–24 mm straight bars, 2–10 mm anchor bars and 6 mm stirrups at 50 cm c/c:

main bars : 3 × 5.00 m × (24²/162) = 3 × 5.00 × 3.556 = 53.3 kg anchor bars : 2 × 5.00 m × (10²/162) = 2 × 5.00 × 0.617 = 6.2 kg stirrups : n = (500/50) + 1 = 11 no. length of one ≈ 2(30 + 62) − 8×cover + hooks weight = 11 × length × (6²/162)

4.3 Septic tank with soak pit

A septic tank is a watertight settling chamber that treats sewage from a building where no public sewer exists; the effluent then percolates into the ground through a soak pit (soakaway).

Sizing

capacity = number of users × sewage per head per day × detention period + sludge storage sewage ≈ 90–135 litres per head per day detention ≈ 24 hours sludge ≈ 30 litres per head per year of cleaning interval free board ≈ 0.30 m above the liquid level length : breadth = 2:1 to 4:1 , liquid depth 1.0–2.0 m

4.4 Underground water tank

An underground water tank (reservoir) is estimated exactly like a septic tank but is designed to hold clean water, so the whole shell is watertight RCC and the internal finish matters.

Sizing

capacity (litres) = number of users × demand per head per day × storage days volume (m³) = capacity / 1000 domestic demand ≈ 100–135 litres per head per day storage ≈ 1 day for a house, 2–3 days for an institution free board ≈ 0.15–0.30 m

Remember 1 m³ = 1000 litres. A tank for 20 people at 135 l/head/day for one day needs 2700 litres = 2.7 m³, plus free board.

Items to be measured

  1. Earthwork in excavation — outside dimensions plus working space, to the full depth; classed by soil, and separately below water table if met.
  2. PCC levelling course under the base slab.
  3. RCC base slab — plan area × thickness.
  4. RCC walls — centre-line perimeter × thickness × height.
  5. RCC top slab, with the manhole opening deducted.
  6. Reinforcement for base, walls and top, from the bar schedule.
  7. Formwork to the walls (both faces) and to the soffit of the top slab — measured in m².
  8. Waterproof cement plaster inside, and plaster outside.
  9. Manhole cover, ladder / rungs, vent pipe, inlet, outlet, overflow and washout pipes — counted or in running metre.
  10. Backfilling around the tank in layers, and disposal of surplus earth.
volume of walls = centre-line perimeter × thickness × height for an internal size L × B and wall thickness t : centre-line perimeter = 2[(L + t) + (B + t)]

Note the difference from a building: for a tank the internal dimensions are fixed by the capacity, so the centre-line perimeter is found by adding one wall thickness to each internal dimension — the mirror image of the crossing method in §2.3.

Dawadi, 1 km highway, RCC water tank and building.
