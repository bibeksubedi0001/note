One-way & Two-way Slabs; Longitudinally Loaded Stairs (IS 456:2000)
Covers the classification and design of one-way and two-way slabs (including the IS 456 moment coefficients and corner torsion reinforcement), slab detailing, and the design of longitudinally spanning staircases.

5.1 INTRODUCTION & CLASSIFICATION

A slab is a plate element carrying loads mainly by flexure. With ly = longer span and lx = shorter span:

  • One-way slab (lylx > 2): bends primarily in the short direction; main steel along lx, distribution steel along ly.
  • Two-way slab (lylx ≤ 2): bends in both directions; main steel in both directions.

5.2 ONE-WAY SLAB DESIGN

Designed as a beam of 1 m width:

  1. Depth from deflection: d = lxbasic Ld × modification. Basic Ld = 20 (simply supported), 26 (continuous), 7 (cantilever).
  2. Loads: wu = 1.5(dead + live + floor finish) per m².
  3. Moment: Mu = wulx²8 (SS) or from IS 456 continuous-slab coefficients (Table 12).
  4. Main steel Ast from Mu (per m width); spacing ≤ 3d or 300 mm.
  5. Distribution steel = 0.12% of gross area (HYSD) or 0.15% (mild steel); spacing ≤ 5d or 450 mm.
  6. Checks: shear (usually safe), deflection (Ld), and development length.

5.3 TWO-WAY SLAB DESIGN

Moments are found using the IS 456 Annex D coefficients based on the edge conditions and the aspect ratio lylx:

Mx = αx·wu·lx²  and  My = αy·wu·lx², where αx, αy are the negative/positive moment coefficients (Table 26 for restrained slabs, Table 27 for simply supported).

  1. Assume depth from Ld (short span); compute loads.
  2. Read αx, αy for the panel type; find Mx, My at mid-span and supports.
  3. Design steel in both directions (short-span bars placed in the lower layer for maximum d).
  4. Torsion (corner) reinforcement: at corners where the slab is discontinuous, provide top and bottom mesh = ¾ of the mid-span steel, over lx5 each way (restrained slabs with corners held down).
  5. Check shear and deflection.

5.4 SLAB DETAILING

  • Minimum steel = 0.12% (HYSD) / 0.15% (mild) of gross area.
  • Maximum bar spacing = 3d or 300 mm (main), 5d or 450 mm (distribution).
  • Maximum bar diameter ≤ D8.
  • Alternate bars bent up near supports, or separate top bars over continuous supports (0.15–0.25 of span).

5.5 STAIRCASE DESIGN

A longitudinally spanning stair (waist-slab type) is designed as a one-way slab spanning between supports at the ends of the flight.

  • Effective span (IS 456 cl. 33): depends on how the landings are supported — generally the going plus half of each landing, or the clear horizontal span plus supports.
  • Loads: self-weight of the waist slab (on the slope, converted to plan), self-weight of steps (½×rise×γ), floor finish and live load. On the landing, the slab is horizontal.
  • Moment: Mu = wuleff²8 (simply supported flight); design main steel along the span and distribution steel across.
  • Provide reinforcement at re-entrant corners; bend main bars to avoid the tension zone bursting at the kink.
