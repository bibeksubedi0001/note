4.1 Flexural Behaviour of Reinforced Concrete

Bending is resisted by an internal compression force and an equal tensile force separated by a lever arm. In a sagging beam the top is normally in compression and the bottom in tension; near a continuous support under hogging moment the roles reverse. Reinforcement must follow the tension face of the actual moment envelope, not a rule that all main bars belong at the bottom.

At low loads the section is nearly elastic and uncracked. After flexural cracking, steel carries the tension across cracks and stiffness reduces. As loading increases, a properly proportioned tension-controlled section develops large steel strain and rotation before concrete crushing. Brittle shear, bond, compression or joint failures must be prevented if that flexural ductility is to develop.

Equilibrium, Compatibility and Material Law

  1. Equilibrium: for pure bending without axial load, C = T and M = Cz = Tz.
  2. Compatibility: plane sections remain plane. At a flexural compression strain of 0.0035, εst = 0.0035(d − xu)/xu.
  3. Material response: obtain concrete and steel stresses from the relevant design curves. Do not assume a compression bar develops 0.87fy without checking its strain.

The IS 456 parabolic-rectangular concrete distribution has resultant C = 0.36fckbxu at 0.42xu from the compression face. The peak stress 0.446fck is not the average stress over xu. For a section satisfying the tensile-strain requirement, T = 0.87fyAst and z = d − 0.42xu.

Complete Singly Reinforced Design Procedure

  1. Establish the effective span, support conditions and factored bending-moment envelope. Add beam self-weight only when excluded from the stated loading.
  2. Select width, overall depth, exposure cover and a trial bar arrangement. Derive the actual effective depth to the centroid of all tension layers.
  3. Calculate xu,max for the steel grade and Mu,lim = 0.36fckbxu,max(d − 0.42xu,max).
  4. When Mu ≤ Mu,lim, solve Mu = 0.36fckbxu(d − 0.42xu) for its smaller physical root, then obtain Ast = 0.36fckbxu/(0.87fy).
  5. Apply minimum steel Ast,min = 0.85bd/fy and ordinary maximum tension steel 0.04bD. Select actual bars, then recheck xu and capacity using the provided area.
  6. Check that the bars physically fit with the required clear horizontal and vertical spacing. A second layer reduces effective depth and changes capacity.
  7. Complete shear, anchorage, curtailment, serviceability and detailing checks. A flexural calculation alone is not a complete beam design.

When the moment is too large: a doubly reinforced solution adds a compression-steel couple; a deeper or wider section may be more practical. The dimensions given in an exam question should not silently be changed. If an alternative size is proposed, distinguish it from a solution for the specified section.

Doubly Reinforced Design and the Displaced Concrete Correction

At the code-limiting neutral axis, separate Mu into Mu,lim and an additional moment M2. Compression steel at depth d′ has strain εsc = 0.0035(1 − d′/xu,max). Read fsc from the appropriate design curve. The concrete stress at that same level is fcc; it equals the plateau stress only if the local concrete strain has reached 0.002.

Asc = M2/[(fsc − fcc)(d − d′)]
Ast1 = 0.36fckbxu,max/(0.87fy)
Ast2 = M2/[0.87fy(d − d′)]
Ast = Ast1 + Ast2

The subtraction fsc − fcc prevents counting both steel and the concrete displaced by it. After rounding to actual bars, re-establish equilibrium with the provided compression and tension steel; excess tension steel can move the neutral axis past its permitted limit. Keep compression bars laterally restrained by links.

Q5. Describe the requirement of steel as reinforcement in RCC structures. Explain the moment of resistance of a doubly reinforced section. Derive the formula. [2+2+4] (2076 Chaitra, Q1a)

A doubly-reinforced section carries steel in both the tension and the compression zone. It is used when the applied moment exceeds the limiting (balanced) capacity Mu,lim of a singly-reinforced section but the size cannot be increased. The moment is split into two couples (superposition):

  • Balanced couple — the concrete stress block (with tension steel Ast1) gives Mu,lim = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max).
  • Steel couple — the compression steel Asc (stress fsc − 0.446fck, the concrete it displaces) with matching tension steel Ast2 gives M2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′).

Adding, the moment of resistance is:

Mu = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max) + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′)

Q6. Derive equations for the bending moment carrying capacity of a doubly-reinforced rectangular section using the assumptions for the working stress method given by IS 456:2000. [6] (2079 Bhadra, Q1b)

This question specifically asks for WSM. Use service moment, a triangular elastic concrete stress diagram and the WSM modular ratio; the ultimate 0.36fck block is not its answer. Let compression steel Asc be at depth d′ and tension steel Ast at depth d, with d′ < x < d.

For the code's effective compression-steel modular ratio 1.5m, the added transformed compression area is (1.5m − 1)Asc. The minus one removes the concrete already included at the bar location. First moments about the neutral axis give:

bx²/2 + (1.5m − 1)Asc(x − d′) = mAst(d − x)

Solve this equation for x. The transformed second moment of area is:

Itr = bx³/3 + (1.5m − 1)Asc(x − d′)² + mAst(d − x)²

At a service moment M, the three stresses are fc = Mx/Itr, fst = mM(d − x)/Itr and fsc = 1.5mM(x − d′)/Itr. Therefore the permissible moment of resistance is:

MR = min { σcbcItr/x,   σstItr/[m(d − x)],   σscItr/[1.5m(x − d′)] }

Use the applicable permissible compression stress for steel as well as the concrete and tensile-steel limits. The same result follows by taking moments of the triangular concrete compression force and the net compression-steel force about the tension steel. The smallest of the three moments controls.

Q7. Explain the design steps of a doubly reinforced section with neat sketches. [6] (2080 Baishakh, Q1c)

  1. Compute Mu and Mu,lim = 0.138fckbd² (Fe415). If Mu ≤ Mu,lim → singly; else doubly.
  2. Tension steel for the balanced couple: Ast1 = Mu,lim0.87fy(d − 0.42xu,max).
  3. Extra moment M2 = Mu − Mu,lim; find fsc from εsc = 0.0035(1 − d′/xu,max).
  4. Compression steel Asc = M2(fsc − 0.446fck)(d − d′); matching tension Ast2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc0.87fy.
  5. Total Ast = Ast1 + Ast2; choose bars, check spacing/cover, and detail the compression bars with ties (see Q5 sketch).

Q1. What is the concept behind the typical flange width in T- and L-beams? Explain with necessary sketches. [2+2] (2082 Bhadra, Q2a)

When a slab is cast monolithically with the beam, a portion of the slab acts as the beam's compression flange — an interior beam becomes a T-beam, an edge beam an L-beam. The compressive stress in the flange is not uniform (it falls off away from the web — “shear lag”), so only a limited effective width bf is assumed to carry the compression at a uniform stress. IS 456 gives:

T-beam: bf = l06 + bw + 6Df   ·   L-beam: bf = l012 + bw + 3Df

where l0 = distance between points of zero moment, bw = web width, Df = flange (slab) thickness; bf must not exceed the actual available slab width (beam spacing).

Q2. Describe the design steps of flanged beam sections. [4] (2081 Baishakh, Q2b)

  1. Find the effective flange width bf and the factored moment Mu.
  2. Assume the NA in the flange: xu from 0.36fckbfxu = 0.87fyAst. If xu ≤ Df the section behaves as a rectangular beam of width bf: Mu = 0.87fyAst(d − 0.42xu).
  3. If xu > Df the NA is in the web — split into a flange couple and a web couple, using the equivalent flange depth yf = min(0.15xu + 0.65Df, Df): Mu = 0.36fckbwxu(d − 0.42xu) + 0.45fck(bf − bw)yf(d − yf/2).
  4. Find Ast from equilibrium; check Ast,min; design shear on the web; check deflection.

Q3. Explain how you would design shear reinforcement for flanged beam sections. [5] (2074 Chaitra, Q1a)

In a flanged beam the shear is resisted essentially by the web only (the thin flange contributes little). Hence the nominal shear stress uses the web width: τv = Vubw·d. Compute the concrete capacity τc from the web steel ratio pt = 100Ast/(bwd). If τv ≤ τc provide nominal stirrups; if τc < τv ≤ τc,max the excess Vus = Vu − τcbwd is carried by vertical stirrups, spacing sv = 0.87fyAsvdVus (≤ 0.75d, 300 mm). τv must not exceed τc,max.

Q6. An L-beam has an effective flange width of 925 mm, an effective depth of 450 mm, a flange thickness of 100 mm and a rib breadth of 250 mm. It is reinforced with 4 bars of 20 mm diameter in tension and 3 bars of 16 mm diameter in compression. Find the ultimate moment of resistance of the section at the limit state of collapse. Use M20 concrete and Fe415 steel. [10] (2074 Chaitra, Q4b)

Given: bf = 925 mm, d = 450 mm, Df = 100 mm, bw = 250 mm, Ast = 4−20 φ, Asc = 3−16 φ, d′ = 40 mm, M20, Fe415.  Required: ultimate M.O.R.

Step 1 — Steel areas. Ast = 4×314.2 = 1257 mm²; Asc = 3×201.1 = 603 mm².

Step 2 — Compatible stresses. The question does not specify compression cover, so d′ = 40 mm is an assumption. Solve 0.36fckbfxu + (fsc − fcc)Asc = 0.87fyAst. The local concrete strain is below 0.002, so fcc must come from the parabolic branch rather than the full plateau.

Step 3 — Neutral axis. Iteration gives xu approximately 53.063 mm; the compression steel is elastic at this strain. Use its actual stress and the local displaced-concrete stress in the moment equation.

Step 4 — Verify. xu = 53.2 < Df = 100 ⇒ NA in the flange (rectangular bf).

Step 5 — Moment of resistance. MR = 0.36fckbfxu(d − 0.42xu) + (fsc − fcc)Asc(d − d′) = 192.28 kN·m.

Study answer 192.28 kN·m for the stated 40 mm compression-cover assumption.

Q7. An L-beam has a flange of effective width 900 mm and depth 100 mm; the web below is 250 mm × 500 mm. Determine the reinforcement required if it has to carry a factored bending moment of 615 kN-m and a shear force of 50 kN. Adopt M20 concrete and Fe500 steel. [14] (2072 Kartik, Q4b)

Verified geometry: bf = 900 mm, Df = 100 mm and the web below the flange is 250 × 500 mm. Thus D = 600 mm. Adopt effective covers 50 mm, giving d = 550 mm and d′ = 50 mm. Mu = 615 kN·m, Vu = 50 kN, M20/Fe500.

1. Limiting compression block. xu,max = 253 mm and yf = min(0.15 × 253 + 0.65 × 100, 100) = 100 mm. Evaluate the web and flange couples at this axis; additional compression steel is needed for the specified 615 kN·m.

2. Trial reinforcement and equilibrium. Three 36 mm tension bars give 3053.63 mm², and two 25 mm compression bars give 981.75 mm². Using compatible steel stresses and the flange/web concrete forces, the actual neutral axis is 207.55 mm < 253 mm, and resistance is 651.36 kN·m > 615 kN·m.

3. Web shear. τv = 50000/(250 × 550) = 0.364 MPa. The provided longitudinal area gives adequate concrete shear resistance; satisfy minimum transverse steel with two-legged 8 mm stirrups at 250 mm centres, using 415 MPa as the ordinary shear-design strength cap. This is within the ordinary spacing and minimum-steel limits.

Study result Three 36 mm tension bars and two 25 mm compression bars pass the stated section check at d = 550 mm. Verify nominal cover, fit of the large bars, anchorage and actual torsion/support conditions before final detailing.
