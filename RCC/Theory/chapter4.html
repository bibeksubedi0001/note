Q1. Design a SS beam of effective span 4 m and section 250×475 mm carrying a superimposed load 45 kN/m plus a point load 75 kN at mid-span, for the limit state of collapse in flexure; check deflection. Cover 45, M25, Fe500 (TMT). [10] · 2079 Bhadra, Q1c

Given: b = 250 mm, D = 475 mm, clear cover = 45 mm, span L = 4 m, superimposed UDL = 45 kN/m, point load P = 75 kN at mid-span, M25 (fck = 25), Fe500 (fy = 500).  Required: flexural steel + deflection check.

Step 1 — Effective depth. Assuming 20 mm bars, d = D − cover − ½φ = 475 − 45 − 10 = 420 mm; effective cover to compression steel d′ = 50 mm.

Step 2 — Loads. Self-weight = 0.25×0.475×25 = 2.97 kN/m ⇒ total UDL w = 45 + 2.97 = 47.97 kN/m.

Step 3 — Factored moment. M = wL²8 + PL4 = 47.97×4²8 + 75×44 = 95.9 + 75 = 170.9 kN·m. With γf = 1.5 (IS 456 Cl 36.4.2): Mu = 1.5×170.9 = 256.4 kN·m.

Step 4 — Limiting moment (IS 456 Cl 38.1, Annex G). For Fe500, xu,max/d = 0.46 ⇒ xu,max = 0.46×420 = 193.2 mm. Mu,lim = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max) = 0.36×25×250×193.2×(420 − 81.1) = 147.3 kN·m. Since Mu = 256.4 > 147.3 ⇒ doubly-reinforced.

Step 5 — Tension steel for the balanced couple. Ast1 = Mu,lim0.87fy(d − 0.42xu,max) = 147.3×1060.87×500×338.9 = 999 mm².

Step 6 — Compression-steel stress. Extra moment M2 = Mu − Mu,lim = 256.4 − 147.3 = 109.1 kN·m. Strain εsc = 0.0035(1 − d′/xu,max) = 0.0035(1 − 50/193.2) = 0.00259. From the IS 456 design stress–strain curve for Fe500 (interpolating between ε = 0.00226→391.3 and 0.00277→413.0 N/mm²): fsc = 405 N/mm².

Step 7 — Compression steel. Asc = M2(fsc − 0.446fck)(d − d′) = 109.1×106(405 − 11.15)×370 = 748 mm² → 4−16 φ (804 mm²).

Step 8 — Total tension steel. Ast2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc0.87fy = 393.85×748435 = 678 mm². Ast = Ast1 + Ast2 = 999 + 678 = 1677 mm² → 6−20 φ (1885 mm²).

Step 9 — Deflection check (IS 456 Cl 23.2.1). Basic span/depth for a simply-supported beam = 20. Actual L/d = 4000/420 = 9.5 < 20 ⇒ safe (even before the tension-steel modification factor).

Answer Tension 6−20 φ (1885 mm²) + compression 4−16 φ (804 mm²); deflection safe.

Q2. A beam 300 mm wide with effective depth 500 mm carries a dead load 45 kN/m (excluding self-weight) over a span of 7 m. Find the mid-span steel (LSM). Compression cover 40, M20/Fe415. [15] · 2074 Chaitra, Q1b

Given: b = 300 mm, d = 500 mm, DL = 45 kN/m (excl. self-wt), L = 7 m, d′ = 40 mm, M20 (fck = 20), Fe415 (fy = 415).  Required: mid-span steel.

Step 1 — Loads. Taking overall depth D ≈ 540 mm, self-weight = 0.3×0.54×25 = 4.05 kN/m ⇒ w = 45 + 4.05 = 49.05 kN/m.

Step 2 — Factored moment. M = wL²8 = 49.05×7²8 = 300.4 kN·m ⇒ Mu = 1.5×300.4 = 450.6 kN·m.

Step 3 — Limiting moment. Mu,lim = 0.138fckbd² = 0.138×20×300×500² = 206.9 kN·m < Mu ⇒ doubly-reinforced. xu,max = 0.48×500 = 240 mm.

Step 4 — Balanced couple. Ast1 = Mu,lim0.87fy(d − 0.42xu,max) = 206.9×1060.87×415×399.2 = 1436 mm².

Step 5 — Compression-steel stress. M2 = 450.6 − 206.9 = 243.7 kN·m. εsc = 0.0035(1 − 40/240) = 0.00292 ⇒ from the Fe415 design curve (interpolating 0.00276→351.8 and 0.00380→360.9): fsc = 353 N/mm².

Step 6 — Compression steel. Asc = M2(fsc − 0.446fck)(d − d′) = 243.7×106(353 − 8.92)×460 = 1539 mm² → 5−20 φ (1571 mm²).

Step 7 — Total tension steel. Ast2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc0.87fy = 1467 mm². Ast = 1436 + 1467 = 2903 mm² → 6−25 φ (2945 mm²).

Q4. Design in flexure a 250×300 mm beam simply supported on 300 mm walls 4 m apart, carrying LL 8 kN/m + DL 10 kN/m (including self-weight). M20/Fe415. [8] · 2081 Bhadra, Q2a

Given: b = 250 mm, D = 300 mm, wall width = 300 mm, walls 4 m apart, w = 8 + 10 = 18 kN/m (incl. self-wt), M20, Fe415, cover 40 (d = 260 mm).  Required: flexural steel.

Step 1 — Effective span (IS 456 Cl 22.2). Least of (clear span + d) = (4 − 0.3) + 0.26 = 3.96 m and c/c of supports = 4.0 m ⇒ Leff = 3.96 m.

Step 2 — Factored moment. M = 18×3.96²8 = 35.3 kN·m ⇒ Mu = 1.5×35.3 = 52.9 kN·m.

Step 3 — Limiting moment. Mu,lim = 0.138×20×250×260² = 46.6 kN·m. Mu = 52.9 is only slightly > 46.6 ⇒ the given section is marginally over-limit.

Step 4 — Preferred: deepen the section. Increasing D to ≈ 350 mm (d ≈ 310 mm) gives Mu,lim = 66.3 > 52.9 ⇒ singly-reinforced: Ast = 0.5fckfy[1 − √(1 − 4.6Mu/fckbd²)]bd ≈ 555 mm² → 2−20 φ (628 mm²).

Step 5 — Alternative: keep 250×300 (doubly). Ast1 = 622 mm² (at Mu,lim); M2 = 6.3 kN·m; xu,max = 124.8; εsc = 0.0035(1 − 40/124.8) = 0.00238 ⇒ fsc = 342 N/mm²; Asc = 86 mm² (nominal 2−12), Ast2 = 79 ⇒ Ast = 701 mm² → 3−20 φ (942 mm²).

Answer Deepen to 250×350 → singly, 2−20 φ; or keep 250×300 → doubly, 3−20 φ tension + 2−12 φ compression.

Q5. Explain the moment of resistance of a doubly-reinforced section and derive the formula. [2+2+4] · 2076 Chaitra, Q1a

A doubly-reinforced section carries steel in both the tension and the compression zone. It is used when the applied moment exceeds the limiting (balanced) capacity Mu,lim of a singly-reinforced section but the size cannot be increased. The moment is split into two couples (superposition):

  • Balanced couple — the concrete stress block (with tension steel Ast1) gives Mu,lim = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max).
  • Steel couple — the compression steel Asc (stress fsc − 0.446fck, the concrete it displaces) with matching tension steel Ast2 gives M2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′).

Adding, the moment of resistance is:

Mu = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max) + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′)

Q6. Derive the bending-moment capacity of a doubly-reinforced rectangular section using the assumptions of IS 456:2000. [6] · 2079 Bhadra, Q1b

Assumptions: plane sections remain plane; maximum concrete strain 0.0035; concrete tension neglected; the rectangular-parabolic stress block gives C = 0.36fckb·xu at 0.42xu; steel stresses from the design curve.

Equilibrium (C = T): 0.36fckb·xu + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc = 0.87fyAst ⇒ solve for xu (with fsc read for εsc = 0.0035(1 − d′/xu)).

Taking moments of the two compressive forces about the tension steel:

Mu = 0.36fckb·xu(d − 0.42xu) + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′)

— the balanced-couple term plus the compression-steel couple (see the figure in Q5).

Q7. Explain the design steps of a doubly-reinforced section with sketches. [6] · 2080 Baishakh, Q1c

  1. Compute Mu and Mu,lim = 0.138fckbd² (Fe415). If Mu ≤ Mu,lim → singly; else doubly.
  2. Tension steel for the balanced couple: Ast1 = Mu,lim0.87fy(d − 0.42xu,max).
  3. Extra moment M2 = Mu − Mu,lim; find fsc from εsc = 0.0035(1 − d′/xu,max).
  4. Compression steel Asc = M2(fsc − 0.446fck)(d − d′); matching tension Ast2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc0.87fy.
  5. Total Ast = Ast1 + Ast2; choose bars, check spacing/cover, and detail the compression bars with ties (see Q5 sketch).

Q8. A beam 300×500 mm has 5−25 mm tension + 5−12 mm compression bars at 25 mm cover. Find its moment of resistance at the ultimate limit state. M25/Fe415. [12] · 2071 Chaitra, Q4b

Given: b = 300 mm, D = 500 mm, cover = 25 mm, Ast = 5−25 φ, Asc = 5−12 φ, M25, Fe415.  Required: ultimate M.O.R.

Step 1 — Section properties. d = 500 − 25 − ½(25) = 462.5 mm; d′ = 25 + 6 = 31 mm. Ast = 5×490.9 = 2454 mm²; Asc = 5×113.1 = 565 mm².

Step 2 — Depth of neutral axis (assume both steels yield). xu = 0.87fyAst − (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc0.36fckb = 254.9 mm.

Step 3 — Check ductility (IS 456 Cl 38.1). xu,max = 0.48×462.5 = 222 mm. xu = 255 > 222 ⇒ the section is over-reinforced; the tension steel does not reach 0.87fy, so the usable depth is restricted to xu,max.

Step 4 — Limiting M.O.R. (at xu = xu,max). εsc = 0.0035(1 − 31/222) = 0.00301 ⇒ fsc = 354 N/mm². Mu = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max) + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′) = 221.4 + 83.6 = 305 kN·m.

Answer M.O.R. ≈ 305 kN·m (over-reinforced — excess tension steel above the balanced value is not fully utilised).

Q9. Find the ultimate moment of resistance of a beam of effective section 250×450 mm reinforced with 4−25 mm bars in tension. M20/Fe415. [14] · 2073 Shrawan, Q1b

Given: b = 250 mm, d = 450 mm, Ast = 4−25 φ, M20, Fe415.  Required: ultimate M.O.R.

Step 1 — Tension steel. Ast = 4×490.9 = 1963 mm².

Step 2 — Depth of neutral axis (assume steel yields). xu = 0.87fyAst0.36fckb = 0.87×415×19630.36×20×250 = 393.8 mm.

Step 3 — Check ductility. xu,max = 0.48×450 = 216 mm. xu = 393.8 > 216 ⇒ over-reinforced — the concrete crushes before the steel yields (a brittle, non-permitted section).

Step 4 — Usable M.O.R. IS 456 caps it at Mu,lim = 0.138fckbd² = 0.138×20×250×450² = 139.7 kN·m.

Q1. What is the concept behind the typical flange width in T- and L-beams? Explain with sketches. [2+2] · 2082 Bhadra, Q2a

When a slab is cast monolithically with the beam, a portion of the slab acts as the beam's compression flange — an interior beam becomes a T-beam, an edge beam an L-beam. The compressive stress in the flange is not uniform (it falls off away from the web — “shear lag”), so only a limited effective width bf is assumed to carry the compression at a uniform stress. IS 456 gives:

T-beam: bf = l06 + bw + 6Df   ·   L-beam: bf = l012 + bw + 3Df

where l0 = distance between points of zero moment, bw = web width, Df = flange (slab) thickness; bf must not exceed the actual available slab width (beam spacing).

Q2. Describe the design steps of flanged beam sections. [4] · 2081 Baishakh, Q2a  ·  [5] 2078 Bhadra, Q3c

  1. Find the effective flange width bf and the factored moment Mu.
  2. Assume the NA in the flange: xu from 0.36fckbfxu = 0.87fyAst. If xu ≤ Df the section behaves as a rectangular beam of width bf: Mu = 0.87fyAst(d − 0.42xu).
  3. If xu > Df the NA is in the web — split into a flange couple and a web couple, using the equivalent flange depth yf = min(0.15xu + 0.65Df, Df): Mu = 0.36fckbwxu(d − 0.42xu) + 0.45fck(bf − bw)yf(d − yf/2).
  4. Find Ast from equilibrium; check Ast,min; design shear on the web; check deflection.

Q3. Explain how you would design shear reinforcement for flanged beam sections. [5] · 2074 Chaitra, Q1a

In a flanged beam the shear is resisted essentially by the web only (the thin flange contributes little). Hence the nominal shear stress uses the web width: τv = Vubw·d. Compute the concrete capacity τc from the web steel ratio pt = 100Ast/(bwd). If τv ≤ τc provide nominal stirrups; if τc < τv ≤ τc,max the excess Vus = Vu − τcbwd is carried by vertical stirrups, spacing sv = 0.87fyAsvdVus (≤ 0.75d, 300 mm). τv must not exceed τc,max.

Q4. Determine the limiting moment of resistance and the limiting Ast for a T-beam of flange width 1500, effective depth 590, flange 150 and web 240 mm. M20/Fe415. [6] · 2081 Baishakh, Q2a

Step 2 — Equivalent flange depth. yf = min(0.15xu,max + 0.65Df, Df) = min(0.15×283.2 + 0.65×150, 150) = min(140, 150) = 140 mm.

Answer Mu,lim ≈ 1056 kN·m; Ast,lim ≈ 5752 mm².
