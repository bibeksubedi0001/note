CHAPTER 10 — REINFORCEMENT DETAILING

Every past-exam question of this chapter is stated in full — with its exam sitting (year & session), question number and marks — and answered below.

1.  Discuss the requirements for good detailing. Describe the bar bending schedule. 2079 Bhadra · Q4a [3+3 marks]

Good detailing requires: adequate cover; correct bar spacing, anchorage and laps; curtailment following the bending-moment diagram; ductility (confinement at joints); constructability (room to place and vibrate the concrete); and clear, unambiguous drawings.

A bar bending schedule (BBS) is a tabulated list of every bar — mark, shape/bending code, diameter, cut length, number and total weight — used for estimating the steel quantity and for fabrication/bending on site.

2.  Explain, with sketches, the detailing of RC beams. 2075 Chaitra · Q5b [6 marks]

In a continuous beam the top (tension) steel is placed over the supports (hogging region) and the bottom (tension) steel at mid-span (sagging region). Bars are curtailed following the bending-moment diagram, anchored into the supports by Ld, and stirrups are spaced closer near the supports (where shear is high) and wider at mid-span.

3.  What is curtailment of tension steel in simple beams? Illustrate with a neat sketch. 2069 Chaitra · Q5c [8 marks]

Curtailment is the stopping of tension bars where they are no longer required for moment, since the moment reduces away from the section of maximum moment. To guard against the diagonal crack that may form at the cut-off, each bar must extend beyond its theoretical cut-off point by at least d or 12φ (whichever is greater), and the IS 456 (cl.26.2.3) shear and development-length checks at the cut-off must be satisfied.

Curtailment: the shorter bar is stopped where the moment has reduced, but extended d or 12φ past the theoretical cut-off; the full-length bar continues to the support.

4.  What is splicing and why is it required in RCC structures? Enlist and sketch the kinds of splices. 2076 Chaitra · Q5a 2071 Chaitra · Q1b 2076 Ashwin · Q4b 2068 Baishakh · Q4a 2074 Ashwin · Q3a [2–5]

Splicing joins two bars where the required length exceeds the available (rolled) bar length, or at construction joints, so the force is transferred from one bar to the next. Types:

(i) Lap splice — the bars overlap by the development length Ld (not used for φ > 36 mm); (ii) welded splice; (iii) mechanical couplers — parallel-thread, taper-thread or cold-swaged sleeve. Rules: stagger the splices, avoid points of maximum stress, and splice ≤ 50% of the bars at any one section.

A lap splice (bars overlapped by Ld) and a mechanical (threaded-sleeve) coupler.

CHAPTER 11 — EARTHQUAKE RESISTANT DESIGN & DUCTILE DETAILING

Every past-exam question of this chapter is stated in full — with its exam sitting (year & session), question number and marks — and answered below.

1.  Explain the philosophy of design of structures in an earthquake-prone region. How do you design for strength and ductility? 2080 Baishakh · Q4a [4+4 marks]

The seismic design philosophy is that the structure should suffer no damage in a minor earthquake, repairable damage in a moderate one, and no collapse in a major one. Because designing a building to remain fully elastic under a major quake is uneconomical, it is instead designed for ductility — the ability to deform inelastically and dissipate the seismic energy without collapsing. Strength is provided for the design base shear (IS 1893), while ductility is ensured by capacity design (strong-column / weak-beam) and IS 13920 detailing (confinement, closed hoops, proper anchorage).

2.  What is ductility? How is it induced in reinforced concrete members? 2082 Bhadra 2073 Shrawan 2072 Kartik [4+4 marks]

Ductility is the ability of a member to undergo large inelastic deformation without a significant loss of strength (ductility ratio μ = ΔuΔy). It is induced by: keeping sections under-reinforced; confining the concrete with closely-spaced hoops; limiting the axial stress; adopting the strong-column/weak-beam hierarchy; and designing the shear for the probable moment capacity so that ductile flexural yielding precedes any brittle shear or bond failure.

3.  What are the factors affecting the ductility of a RC section? 2078 Bhadra [4 marks]

Tension-steel ratio (lower → more ductile), compression steel (increases ductility), concrete grade, confinement by hoops, axial load (higher → less ductile), and the steel grade/type.

4.  How is the earthquake load considered in design? 2072 Kartik · Q2a [4 marks]

Per IS 1893 the design base shear is VB = AhW, with Ah = (Z2)(IR)(Sag); it is distributed over the height as storey forces, and the response-reduction factor R rewards ductile detailing. (See Chapter 1 Q11 for the full treatment.)

5.  Explain the ductile detailing of beams, columns and beam-column joints as per IS 13920, with sketches. 2082 Bhadra 2081 Bhadra 2076 Chaitra 2074 Chaitra 2069 Chaitra [6–8]

Solution — Beams: at least 2 bars continuous top and bottom; ρmin = 0.24√fckfy, ρmax = 2.5%; the bottom (positive) capacity at a joint face ≥ ½ of the top (negative) capacity; special confining hoops over 2d from the support face at spacing ≤ d4, 8φ and ≥ 100 mm.

Columns: special confining reinforcement over a length lo = max(larger dimension, clear height6, 450 mm) at each end; hoop spacing ≤ ¼ of the minimum member dimension and 100 mm; applies where the factored axial stress > 0.1fck; the strong-column/weak-beam rule ΣMcol ≥ 1.1ΣMbeam is satisfied.

Beam-column joints: confine the joint core by continuing the column hoops through the joint; anchor the beam bars for Ld + 10φ; check the joint shear against the probable beam moments.

Ductile beam-column joint: closely-spaced confining hoops over lo at the column ends and 2d in the beam, with the joint core confined by continuing the hoops through it.
