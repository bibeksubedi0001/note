CHAPTER 4 — DESIGN FOR FLEXURE

1.  Explain the moment of resistance of a doubly reinforced section and derive the formula. 2076 Chaitra · Q1a [2+2+4 marks]

A doubly-reinforced section carries steel in both the tension and the compression zone. It is used when the applied moment exceeds the limiting capacity Mu,lim of a singly-reinforced section but the size cannot be increased. The moment is split into two couples:

• a balanced (concrete) couple with tension steel Ast1: Mu,lim = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d − 0.42xu,max);
• a steel couple (compression steel Asc balanced by extra tension steel Ast2): Mu2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′).

Adding the two couples, Mu = Mu,lim + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′), with Ast = Ast1 + Ast2 and Ast2 = (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc0.87fy. The compression-steel stress fsc is read from the design curve for the strain εsc = 0.0035(1 − d′xu,max).

Doubly-reinforced section: the moment = balanced concrete couple (Cc) + compression-steel couple (Cs), resisted by the tension steel T.

2.  Derive the bending-moment capacity of a doubly-reinforced rectangular section using the assumptions of IS 456:2000. 2079 Bhadra · Q1b [6 marks]

From equilibrium C = T: 0.36fckb·xu + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc = 0.87fyAst, which gives xu. Taking moments of the compressive forces about the tension steel: Mu = 0.36fckb·xu(d − 0.42xu) + (fsc − 0.446fck)Asc(d − d′) — i.e. the balanced couple plus the compression-steel couple (see the figure in Q1).

12.  What is the concept behind the typical (effective) flange width in T- and L-beams? Explain with sketches. 2082 Bhadra · Q2a [2+2 marks]

A slab cast monolithically with a beam acts with it, so part of the slab forms the compression flange (T at an interior beam, L at an edge beam). The compressive stress in the flange is not uniform — it is highest over the web and reduces away from it (shear lag). For design this real distribution is replaced by a smaller effective flange width bf carrying a uniform stress and giving the same compression:

• T-beam: bf = L06 + bw + 6Df;  • L-beam: bf = L012 + bw + 3Df  (L0 = distance between points of zero moment; bw = web width; Df = flange depth), limited to the actual slab available.

Effective flange width bf of a T-beam (interior) and an L-beam (edge); the flange forms the compression zone at mid-span.

13.  Describe/explain the design steps of flanged (T-/L-) beam sections. 2081 Baishakh · Q2a 2078 Bhadra · Q3c [4/5]

(1) Find the effective flange width bf. (2) Compute Mu. (3) Assume the neutral axis lies in the flange (xu ≤ Df) and design as a rectangular beam of width bf: Ast from Mu = 0.87fyAst(d − 0.42xu). (4) Check xu: if xu ≤ Df the assumption is valid; if xu > Df, recompute with the flange-overhang couple 0.446fck(bf − bw)Df(d − Df2) added. (5) Detail the bars, check shear and deflection.

Analysis rule for the numericals: assume NA in flange (xu ≤ Df) → design as a rectangular beam of width bf; if xu > Df, add the flange-overhang couple.

CHAPTER 5 — SHEAR, TORSION & BOND

1.  Explain the behaviour of concrete under shear with sketches, showing the different conditions. 2078 Bhadra · Q2a [4 marks]

In a loaded beam the combination of flexural stress and shear stress produces diagonal (principal) tension. Near the supports, where shear is large and moment small, this tension acts at about 45° and causes diagonal-tension (shear) cracks; near mid-span, flexural cracks dominate. Because concrete is weak in tension, once τv exceeds the shear strength τc of the concrete, shear reinforcement (vertical stirrups and/or bent-up bars) must carry the excess. 2.  How do bent-up bars contribute to the shear strength of a beam? 2065 Shrawan · Q3a [4 marks]

Some tension bars, no longer needed for moment near the support, are bent up at 45° so they cross the potential diagonal crack. Their vertical component resists shear: Vus = 0.87fyAsv·sinα (α = 45°). IS 456 limits the bent-up contribution to half of the total shear reinforcement, the rest being carried by stirrups.

(4) Check τve = Vebd ≤ τc,max; if τve > τc, provide closed stirrups for the combined shear + torsion. (5) Detail, respecting maximum spacing (0.75d, 300 mm, and x1, x1+y14 for torsion).

Method: τv=Vubd; pt=100Astbd → τc (Table 19). If τv>τc (and ≤τc,max): Vus=Vu−τcbd; spacing sv=0.87fyAsvdVus (≤0.75d, 300 mm).

17.  Derive the formula for the development length (Ld) and explain its use in RCC construction. 2082 Bhadra · Q3a 2081 Bhadra · Q3a 2076 Ashwin · Q2a 2078 Bhadra · Q3b [3+4 marks]

A bar must be embedded a length Ld so that the bond resistance over its surface equals the tensile force it carries. Equating the two:

(π4)φ²·σs = (πφLd)·τbd  ⇒  Ld = φσs4τbd, with σs = 0.87fy and τbd the design bond stress (increased 60% for deformed bars).

The bar's tensile force T is developed by the bond stress τbd acting over the embedded surface πφLd.

Use: Ld is the minimum embedment/lap needed at supports, curtailment points and splices so a bar develops its full strength without pulling out. For Fe415 in M20 (τbd=1.92): Ld=47φ → 16 mm=752 mm, 20 mm=940 mm, 25 mm=1175 mm.

18.  Define anchorage bond and flexural bond stress. Prove that the flexural bond stress is a function of the shear force, and derive Ld ≤ 1.3M1Vu + Lo. 2080 Bhadra · Q2a 2075 Ashwin · Q1b 2074 Ashwin · Q2b 2079 Bhadra · Q3a 2072 Kartik · Q3a [7+8 marks]

Anchorage (development) bond is the average bond over the anchorage length that resists pull-out of a bar (τbd = σsφ4Ld). Flexural (local) bond is the bond at a section due to the rate of change of the bar force along the span: τbd = VΣo·z, where Σo = total bar perimeter and z = lever arm. Since dTdx = Vz, the flexural bond stress is directly proportional to the shear force V.

At a simply-supported end the bars must develop the moment within the available anchorage, giving IS 456's rule Ld ≤ 1.3M1Vu + Lo (M1 = MOR of the bars continued into the support, Vu = shear at the support, Lo = anchorage beyond the support centre). The factor 1.3 applies when the ends are confined by a compressive reaction.

CHAPTER 6 — SERVICEABILITY: DEFLECTION & CRACKING

Every past-exam question of this chapter is stated in full — with its exam sitting (year & session), question number and marks — and answered below.

1.  How can deflection be controlled in a beam? Explain briefly. 2065 Shrawan · Q2a [4 marks]

Deflection is kept within the permissible limit (span250) mainly by limiting the span/effective-depth ratio (IS 456 cl.23.2), i.e. providing enough depth/stiffness. It is further reduced by: providing compression steel; controlling the tension-steel percentage (lower pt and lower service stress raise the allowable Ld); using a higher grade of concrete (larger Ec); and cambering long-span members.

2.  Explain the principle of the sufficient-stiffness method to control deflection; how is the deflection of a RC flexural member controlled? 2065 Kartik · Q3a [6 marks]

Rather than computing the actual deflection, IS 456 keeps it within limits indirectly by ensuring the member is stiff enough — the span/effective-depth ratio is capped. The basic Ld (7 cantilever, 20 simply supported, 26 continuous) is multiplied by modification factors: k2 for tension steel (from the service stress fs=0.58fy·Ast,reqAst,prov and pt), k3 for compression steel, and k4 for flanged sections. If the provided Ld is below (basic×k2k3k4), the deflection is automatically within the allowable value — no detailed calculation is needed.

3.  Describe the method of controlling deflection and cracking in RCC structures. 2076 Chaitra · Q2a [2+4 marks]

Deflection is controlled by the span/depth-ratio (stiffness) method above. Cracking is controlled by: limiting the bar spacing and diameter (many small bars rather than few large ones); providing minimum tension steel (Ast,min=0.85bdfy) and side-face steel in deep beams; adequate cover; and keeping the surface crack width ≤ 0.3 mm (mild exposure) using the IS 456 Annex-F formula.

4.  State the methods of controlling deflection and crack width, and give the empirical formula for the design surface crack width. 2076 Ashwin · Q5c [3+2 marks]

Methods — as in Q1–Q3 (Ld ratio + modification factors for deflection; bar spacing/diameter, minimum steel and cover for cracking). The IS 456 (Annex F) surface crack width is:

wcr = 3acrεm / [1 + 2acr − CminD − x], where acr = distance to the nearest bar surface, εm = average strain at the level considered, Cmin = minimum cover, x = neutral-axis depth.

5.  Discuss the methods of crack control as per IS 456:2000. 2073 Shrawan · Q3b [5 marks]

IS 456 controls cracking by: (i) limiting the clear spacing of tension bars; (ii) limiting the bar diameter; (iii) providing minimum tension reinforcement and side-face reinforcement (for webs deeper than 750 mm); (iv) adequate cover and good compaction/curing; and (v) where required, a direct check of the surface crack width against the 0.3 mm limit using the Annex-F formula.
