6.1 DAMAGE TO RCC IN EARTHQUAKES

Common failure modes observed in RC buildings during earthquakes:

  • Soft-storey collapse — open ground storey (parking) with far less stiffness than upper storeys.
  • Short-column effect — partial infill / mezzanines shorten columns, attracting large shear → brittle shear failure.
  • Strong-beam / weak-column — plastic hinges form in columns, leading to storey mechanism and collapse.
  • Beam-column joint failure — inadequate joint confinement and anchorage.
  • Pounding between adjacent buildings, torsion from plan irregularity, and foundation failure.

6.2 DESIGN PHILOSOPHY IN SEISMIC REGIONS

Performance objectives (IS 1893)
  • Minor (frequent) shaking — no damage; structure remains elastic.
  • Moderate shaking — minor, repairable non-structural damage.
  • Major (rare) shaking — no collapse; structural damage acceptable but life is protected.

Since designing structures to remain elastic under a major earthquake is uneconomical, the accepted philosophy is to design for ductility — allow the structure to deform inelastically and dissipate energy without collapse, using capacity-design principles (strong column – weak beam).

6.3 DESIGN FOR STRENGTH & DUCTILITY

Ductility
The ability of a material, section or structure to undergo large inelastic deformation without a significant loss of strength, giving warning before failure and dissipating seismic energy. Measured by the ductility ratio μ = ΔuΔy (ultimate to yield deformation).

How ductility is induced: use under-reinforced sections (steel yields first), confine concrete with closely-spaced hoops, limit axial stress in columns, ensure the strong-column/weak-beam hierarchy, provide adequate anchorage and avoid brittle shear/bond failures (design shear for the probable moment capacity, not just the factored moment).

6.4 DUCTILITY PROVISIONS IN CODES

IS 1893 gives the seismic loads (base shear VB = Ah·W, where Ah = ZI Sa/(2Rg)), and IS 13920 gives the ductile-detailing rules for members of the lateral-load-resisting system. The response-reduction factor R (e.g. 5 for a Special Moment-Resisting Frame) directly rewards ductile detailing with lower design forces.

6.5 DUCTILE DETAILING: BEAM, COLUMN & JOINT (IS 13920)

Beams

  • At least 2 bars continuous top and bottom throughout the length.
  • Tension steel ratio ρmin = 0.24√fckfy; ρmax = 2.5%.
  • Positive (bottom) moment capacity at a joint face ≥ ½ of the negative (top) capacity.
  • Special confining hoops over 2d from the face of the support; spacing ≤ d4, 8× smallest bar diameter, and not < 100 mm.

Columns

  • Applies where the factored axial stress > 0.1fck.
  • Special confining reinforcement over a length lo (≥ larger dimension, l6, 450 mm) at each end; hoop spacing ≤ ¼ of minimum member dimension and 100 mm.
  • Strong-column/weak-beam: ΣMcolumn ≥ 1.1×ΣMbeam at every joint (IS 13920:2016).

Beam-Column Joints

  • Confine the joint core with hoops (continue column confining steel through the joint).
  • Provide adequate anchorage of beam bars into the joint (development length + 10φ).
  • Check joint shear against the design shear from the beam probable moments.
