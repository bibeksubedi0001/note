9.1 Design of Spread Footing 2065

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Design a reinforced-concrete spread footing for a reinforced-concrete wall 4 m long and 300 mm wide. The wall carries a total service load of 1000 kN. The safe bearing capacity of the soil is 150 kN/m². Use M20 concrete and Fe415 steel. [14] (2065 Kartik, Q4a)
  2. Describe the steps for the design of a rectangular RC footing. Why is shear reinforcement not provided in footings? [4] (2065 Shrawan, Q4a)

A footing spreads the column/wall load onto the soil so the bearing pressure stays within the safe bearing capacity (SBC). Shear reinforcement is normally avoided in footings because it is difficult to place and anchor in a relatively shallow, wide member; instead the depth is chosen large enough that the concrete alone resists both one-way and two-way (punching) shear — a deeper footing is cheaper and simpler than adding stirrups.

The foundation must satisfy two different groups of requirements: geotechnical performance, including bearing resistance, settlement, sliding and overturning; and structural resistance, including bending, shear, punching, anchorage and transfer of column/wall forces. A plan area based on SBC alone is not a complete footing design.

Service Loads, Factored Loads, Gross and Net Pressure

When the problem supplies an allowable/SBC value, compare it with the appropriate service soil pressure. Use factored actions for concrete and reinforcement design. If a column load is already factored, do not factor it again. Dividing an ultimate load by 1.5 to recover service load is justified only when that is the actual governing load factor and combination.

Gross pressure includes column load, footing weight and soil/backfill above the base. Net allowable pressure is referenced to the original overburden state. Establish which type the quoted bearing value represents; do not subtract overburden from a net value a second time. A 10% allowance for footing and backfill is only a preliminary sizing estimate and must be replaced by actual weights after choosing depth.

For a centred rectangular footing with uniform weight and a consistent gravity factor, the structural net upward pressure can be taken as qu,net = Pu,column/A: the upward reaction due to the uniformly distributed footing/backfill weight cancels the corresponding downward load in slab bending. The gross service-pressure check still includes those weights.

Design steps

  1. Plan area: make a preliminary service-load/SBC calculation, choose dimensions, then recalculate actual self-weight, soil cover, eccentricity and pressure. Check the geotechnical report's gross/net convention.
  2. Structural loading: derive the net factored upward pressure from the correct load combination. Maintain consistent units and keep the downward column force in the equilibrium model.
  3. Depth from shear: check one-way shear at d from the face and two-way (punching) shear on the perimeter at d/2; increase d until τv ≤ the permissible value without stirrups.
  4. Flexure: M at the column face = pu·(cantilever projection)²2 → Ast; check development length of the bars beyond the critical section.

Wall or Strip Footing

Design a reinforced-concrete spread footing for a reinforced-concrete wall 4 m long and 300 mm wide. The wall carries a total service load of 1000 kN. The safe bearing capacity of the soil is 150 kN/m². Use M20 concrete and Fe415 steel. [14] (2065 Kartik, Q4a)

A continuous strip footing under a uniformly loaded wall is designed per metre length. For the 2065 wall question, the service line load is 1000/4 = 250 kN/m. A preliminary 10% weight allowance gives B = 1.10 × 250/150 = 1.833 m, so a trial 1.9 m width may be used before the final weight/pressure check. The projection on each side of the 300 mm wall is (B − 0.3)/2.

Transverse bottom reinforcement carries bending of the projections from the wall face. Check one-way shear at the applicable distance from the face and provide longitudinal distribution steel. A uniformly loaded continuous wall normally does not produce the same isolated-column punching mechanism. End regions, discontinuous wall loads and openings require their own consideration.

  18. Design an isolated square footing of uniform thickness for a 400 mm × 400 mm column subjected to axial load 600 kN and moment 50 kN·m at service state. Take allowable soil bearing pressure 150 kN/m², M20 concrete and Fe415 steel. [10] (2067 Ashadh, Q2b)

An isolated footing supports one column. With a moment about one axis and full soil contact, pressure varies linearly: q = P/A ± M/Z. For a base B wide and L long in the direction of eccentricity, Z = BL²/6, e = M/P and qmax,min = P/(BL)[1 ± 6e/L]. Check both qmax ≤ SBC and qmin ≥ 0 before using this full-contact distribution.

If e > L/6, the linear formula predicts tension, which ordinary soil contact cannot carry. Recalculate with partial contact or revise the foundation. For one-axis eccentricity with L/6 < e < L/2, an ideal triangular compression block has contact length a = 3(L/2 − e) and qmax = 2P/(Ba). For biaxial loading evaluate all four corner pressures, not just separate one-axis checks.

Critical Sections and Their Derivations

For a centred square footing of side B beneath a square column of side c, the projection is a = (B − c)/2. Under uniform net factored pressure qu, the bending moment per metre strip at the column face is Mu = qua²/2, obtained by integrating the upward load times its lever arm. For a rectangular footing, use the appropriate projection and bar-layer depth in each direction.

One-way shear per metre strip at distance d from the face is Vu = qu(a − d), provided the section lies within the footing. The punching perimeter is at d/2 from the column faces. For a rectangular interior column, b0 = 2(c1 + c2 + 2d), and the net punching force is the column load minus upward pressure inside the perimeter. Edge/corner columns and eccentric moment transfer need modified assessment.

Full Worked Study Design: 2082 Bhadra

Design an isolated footing for a column of size 500 mm × 500 mm carrying a service axial load of 1600 kN. The allowable bearing capacity of the soil is 125 kN/m² at a depth of 1.25 m below ground level. Use Fe500 TMT steel in both the column and footing. Use M25 concrete in both the footing and the column. Prepare construction drawings in plan and elevation. [10+2+2] (2082 Bhadra, Q5a)

Paper data: 500 × 500 mm column, service axial load 1600 kN, allowable soil pressure 125 kN/m² at depth 1.25 m, M25 concrete and Fe500 steel. Study assumptions: the allowable pressure is gross, the column load acts at footing level, groundwater is below the base, soil unit weight is 18 kN/m³, concrete unit weight is 25 kN/m³, and the footing is cast on levelling concrete with 50 mm nominal bottom cover.

1. Choose and verify the base. Adopt 4.10 × 4.10 m, A = 16.81 m², and a trial overall thickness D = 775 mm. Soil cover above the footing is 1.25 − 0.775 = 0.475 m. Conservatively treating that cover as uniform:

qservice,gross = 1600/16.81 + 25 × 0.775 + 18 × 0.475
= 95.181 + 19.375 + 8.550 = 123.11 kN/m² < 125

A 3.8 m base selected using only a preliminary 10% weight allowance would not satisfy this same final-weight check. Plan size and thickness must be iterated together.

2. Obtain net structural loading. Pu = 1.5 × 1600 = 2400 kN and qu,net = 2400/16.81 = 142.772 kN/m². Projection a = (4.10 − 0.50)/2 = 1.80 m. For two layers of 16 mm bottom bars, depths are 717 mm and 701 mm; use the conservative common design depth d = 700 mm.

3. Flexure per metre width. Mu = 142.772 × 1.80²/2 = 231.291 kN·m/m. Section equilibrium gives xu = 37.559 mm and Ast,required = 777.09 mm²/m. Minimum HYSD steel is 0.0012 × 1000 × 775 = 930 mm²/m, so provide 16 mm bars at 200 mm centres each way, Ast = 1005.31 mm²/m.

4. One-way shear. At d = 0.70 m from the column face, Vu = 142.772(1.80 − 0.70) = 157.049 kN/m. Nominal stress = 157049/(1000 × 700) = 0.224 MPa. At the provided steel percentage, a conservative M25 Table 19 value of 0.29 MPa exceeds this stress. No beneficial thin-slab factor is used.

5. Punching shear. The critical square side is c + d = 1.20 m, so b0 = 4.80 m. Force outside the perimeter is Vp = 2400 − 142.772 × 1.20² = 2194.408 kN. Hence τp = 2194408/(4800 × 700) = 0.653 MPa, below 0.25√25 = 1.25 MPa for the square column.

6. Flexural anchorage. For 16 mm Fe500 deformed bars in M25 tension, Ld = 16 × 435/(4 × 1.4 × 1.6) = 776.79 mm. Approximately 1750 mm is available from the column face toward the edge before nominal end cover, so the straight projection is adequate for this check. Also check the other relevant bar sections and edge termination.

7. Column-footing bearing. The factored bearing stress at the column base is 2400000/(500 × 500) = 9.60 MPa, below the unenhanced 0.45fck = 11.25 MPa. Minimum continued column steel or dowels are still required. The ordinary minimum dowel area is 0.5% of the supported column area = 1250 mm², with at least four bars.

An illustrative eight-16 mm starter arrangement provides 1608.50 mm². Its compression development length in M25 is approximately 621.43 mm after the 25% bond-stress increase, which can be accommodated within this footing. The actual starter sizes, column lap and anchorage must match the column's designed bars; the paper does not specify its longitudinal cage.

Study arrangement: 4.10 m square, 775 mm overall thickness, 16 mm bottom bars at no more than 200 mm centres each way. With 50 mm nominal side cover and 16 mm bars, 21 bars each way allow approximately 199 mm centre spacing. The calculations establish the stated gravity checks under the assumptions above, not site settlement, seismic load transfer or a construction approval.

Eccentric Footing: Preliminary Pressure Check for 2081 Bhadra

Design an isolated footing for a column of size 400 mm × 400 mm reinforced with 8 longitudinal bars of 25 mm diameter. The column carries a service axial load of 1200 kN and a service bending moment of 120 kN·m. Take the safe bearing capacity of the soil as 150 kN/m². Use M20 concrete and Fe415 steel. [12] (2081 Bhadra, Q5b)

For P = 1200 kN and M = 120 kN·m, try B = 3.2 m and L = 3.4 m in the moment direction. A preliminary 10% uniform weight allowance gives Ptotal = 1320 kN, qaverage = 1320/10.88 = 121.324 kPa and M/Z = 6 × 120/(3.2 × 3.4²) = 19.464 kPa. Thus qmax = 140.79 kPa and qmin = 101.86 kPa, provisionally satisfying a gross 150 kPa limit.

Replace that weight allowance after choosing thickness and embedment. Then integrate the nonuniform net factored pressure over each projection to obtain bending and shear; applying the average pressure to both sides ignores the eccentric moment. Check punching and moment transfer at the column, bearing, anchorage and the final bar layout.

Older-paper qualification: the 2080 Bhadra question expressly specifies M15. Preserve that wording when solving the historical paper, but do not infer that M15 is an acceptable default for new reinforced-concrete construction under IS 456:2000, whose ordinary minimum RC grade is M20.

9.3 Combined Footings and Eccentric Rectangular Footings 2072

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Design a rectangular footing to carry an axial column load of 1150 kN and a bending moment of 250 kN·m from a square column of size 600 mm × 600 mm. The column has longitudinal reinforcement of 20 mm diameter. The bearing capacity of the soil is 200 kN/m². Take the foundation depth as 1.5 m and the unit weight of earth as 17 kN/m³. Use M20 concrete and Fe415 steel. [16] (2072 Kartik, Q1b)

A combined footing supports two or more columns on a common base. It is useful when individual footings overlap, an exterior column is close to a property boundary, or column loads must be balanced across one foundation. The 2072 question above is a single-column rectangular footing carrying moment; rectangular shape or eccentricity alone does not make a footing a combined footing.

  1. Find the service-load resultant: xR = ΣPixi/ΣPi, including applied moments and foundation loads consistently.
  2. Choose a rectangular or trapezoidal base whose area centroid is suitably aligned with that resultant. For a rectangular base and uniform pressure, the resultant should pass through its centre.
  3. Verify maximum/minimum service pressures and settlement. Boundary restrictions may require an eccentric design or a strap footing instead of a centred base.
  4. Analyse the base longitudinally under upward soil reaction and the concentrated downward column loads. Construct shear-force and bending-moment diagrams; both top and bottom tension regions may occur.
  5. Design the longitudinal reinforcement, transverse cantilever strips, one-way shear, punching at each column, and column-force transfer.
  6. Detail anchorage across column regions and at edges; coordinate construction joints and reinforcement congestion.

Strap footing distinction: a strap connects separate pads to transfer the eccentric effect of an edge column. A conventional strap is normally designed without assuming soil support beneath the strap itself; that modelling choice must be stated.

9.4 Design of Mat Foundation 208120752069

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Describe the design procedure for a mat foundation. [4] (2081 Bhadra, Q5a)
  2. Describe the design procedure for a mat foundation. [5] (2075 Ashwin, Q4b)
  3. Explain in detail all the design steps of RCC mat foundation design. [6] (2069 Chaitra, Q3a)

A mat or raft is a large foundation supporting several columns or walls. It is considered when loads are high, allowable soil pressure is low, separate footings would overlap, or differential settlement control favours a common base. A high footing-area fraction is an economic screening consideration, not a universal code threshold.

  1. Find the total load ΣP and locate its resultant; proportion the mat so the resultant is at the centroid (uniform pressure p = ΣPA + MZ ≤ SBC).
  2. Analyse the mat as an inverted floor loaded by the (upward) soil pressure — by the conventional rigid method (strips) or the flexible (beam-on-elastic-foundation) method.
  3. Design each strip for the resulting moments and shears; check punching shear around every column; provide top and bottom mesh.

Full Mat-Foundation Design Answer

Start with geotechnical data, groundwater level, allowable bearing pressure, total/differential settlement limits and the complete column/wall load combinations. Fix the mat's extent and trial thickness, calculate its weight and locate the resultant of vertical loads and overturning moments. Check bearing, uplift, sliding and overturning as applicable.

A conventional rigid-base method is appropriate only when the relative rigidity and column/load distribution justify it. A flexible mat may need plate or finite-element analysis with a justified soil model. The subgrade modulus is not a universal soil constant; its value and scale dependence need geotechnical input. Prevent tensile soil reactions in the model unless a real hold-down system is provided.

Obtain moment and shear envelopes in both directions, design top and bottom reinforcement, and check punching at interior, edge and corner columns, openings and concentrated wall loads. Revise thickness or use suitable local thickening if required. Finally check cracking, settlement compatibility, construction joints, temperature/shrinkage reinforcement, waterproofing and anchorage. Draw both reinforcement mats and all local strengthening zones.

Punching Shear: Additional Past-Exam Question 2067

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Write down the procedure for the design of shear reinforcement. Also explain how isolated footings are designed under punching shear. [8] (2067 Ashadh, Q3a)

Punching (two-way) shear is the tendency of the column to punch through the slab along a pyramid whose critical perimeter is at d2 from the column face (perimeter b0 = 2(c1 + c2 + 2d) for a rectangular column). The nominal punching stress

τv = Vub0·d ≤ ks·0.25√fck,   ks = (0.5 + βc) ≤ 1

Here βc is the shorter-to-longer dimension ratio of the column. For a centred interior column under uniform net pressure, Vu equals pressure times the area outside the critical perimeter. With nonuniform pressure, integrate the actual pressure outside it. If the stress exceeds the concrete-only limit, increase depth or use an appropriately designed alternative. Either one-way shear, punching, anchorage, flexure or practical detailing can govern the final thickness.

9.5 Introduction to Pile Caps: Basic Concepts

A pile cap is a reinforced-concrete member that collects column or wall forces and distributes them to a group of piles. Piles transfer load to deeper soil or rock through shaft resistance, end bearing or both. Unlike a spread footing, a cap receives discrete pile reactions, not an automatically uniform soil reaction beneath its whole area.

Load Distribution and Structural Action

For a rigid cap with identical vertical piles at symmetric coordinates, a preliminary elastic reaction model is Ri = P/n + Mxyi/Σy² + Myxi/Σx², with signs determined by pile location. This formula assumes equal axial stiffness and uncoupled symmetric axes; unequal piles, irregular groups, lateral loading and cap flexibility require a more complete analysis.

Check each pile reaction against its allowable service resistance and settlement criteria. A negative reaction requires a pile and connection designed for tension; it cannot be ignored. Establish the pile spacing, cap edge distance, pile-head embedment and connection requirements from the applicable pile-design provisions.

Deep caps often transfer force by compression struts from the column to pile heads and tension ties between pile regions. Design struts, nodal zones and fully anchored tie reinforcement using an applicable strut-and-tie method. Where beam/slab theory is applicable, check flexure and one-way/punching shear using the actual pile locations. A pile reaction intersected by a critical section cannot be treated automatically as a uniform soil-pressure area.

Essential Detailing

  • Concentrate the principal tie reinforcement along the load paths linking pile heads, with full anchorage beyond the relevant nodes.
  • Provide distribution, top and side reinforcement required for cracking, local actions and construction integrity.
  • Develop column bars into the cap and provide the designed pile-head connection and confinement.
  • Check cover, congestion, concrete placement and the clear space around pile heads before issuing the drawing.
