9.1 Design of Spread Footing 2065

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. RC spread footing for a RC wall 4 m long, 300 mm wide; total service load 1000 kN. M20/Fe415, SBC 150. [14] (2065 Kartik, Q4a)
  2. Steps for the design of a rectangular RC footing. Why is shear reinforcement not provided in footings? [4] (2065 Shrawan, Q4a)

A footing spreads the column/wall load onto the soil so the bearing pressure stays within the safe bearing capacity (SBC). Shear reinforcement is normally avoided in footings because it is difficult to place and anchor in a relatively shallow, wide member; instead the depth is chosen large enough that the concrete alone resists both one-way and two-way (punching) shear — a deeper footing is cheaper and simpler than adding stirrups.

Design steps

  1. Plan area A = service load (incl. ≈10% self-wt)SBC; choose L×B (square for axial, rectangular/proportioned for moment).
  2. Factored soil pressure pu = 1.5×axialA (net, upward on the footing).
  3. Depth from shear: check one-way shear at d from the face and two-way (punching) shear on the perimeter at d/2; increase d until τv ≤ the permissible value without stirrups.
  4. Flexure: M at the column face = pu·(cantilever projection)²2 → Ast; check development length of the bars beyond the critical section.

9.3 Combined / Rectangular Footings with Moment 2072

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Rectangular footing, column load 1150 kN + BM 250 kN-m from 600×600 with 20 mm bars. SBC 200; depth 1.5 m; unit wt 17. M20/Fe415. [16] (2072 Kartik, Q1b)

When a footing carries a large moment (or two columns share one footing), a rectangular / combined footing is proportioned so that the resultant passes through the centroid of the base — making the pressure as uniform as possible. Size the base for pmax = PA + MZ ≤ SBC, then design the base slab for the (now longitudinal) bending and the projections for transverse bending, checking one-way and punching shear as before.

9.4 Design of Mat Foundation 208120752069

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Describe the design procedure for a mat foundation. [4] (2081 Bhadra, Q5a; 2075 Ashwin, Q4b)
  2. Explain all the design steps of a RCC mat (raft) foundation. [6] (2069 Chaitra, Q3a)

A mat (raft) is one large slab under all the columns, used when the SBC is low or individual footings would overlap (> ~50% of the plan area). Procedure:

  1. Find the total load ΣP and locate its resultant; proportion the mat so the resultant is at the centroid (uniform pressure p = ΣPA + MZ ≤ SBC).
  2. Analyse the mat as an inverted floor loaded by the (upward) soil pressure — by the conventional rigid method (strips) or the flexible (beam-on-elastic-foundation) method.
  3. Design each strip for the resulting moments and shears; check punching shear around every column; provide top and bottom mesh.

9.5 Punching Shear in Footings 2067

PAST EXAM QUESTIONS:

  1. Procedure for the design of shear reinforcement. How are isolated footings designed under punching shear? [8] (2067 Ashadh, Q3b)

Punching (two-way) shear is the tendency of the column to punch through the slab along a pyramid whose critical perimeter is at d2 from the column face (perimeter b0 = 2(c1 + c2 + 2d) for a rectangular column). The nominal punching stress

τv = Vub0·d ≤ ks·0.25√fck,   ks = (0.5 + βc) ≤ 1

where Vu = pu×(area outside the critical perimeter) and βc is the column aspect ratio. If τv exceeds the limit, the depth is increased (shear reinforcement is avoided in footings). This punching check almost always governs the footing thickness.
