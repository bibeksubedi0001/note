4.1 INTRODUCTION & TYPES

A footing transfers column/wall loads to the soil, spreading them so the bearing pressure stays within the safe bearing capacity (SBC). Types:

  • Isolated / spread footing — one column (square, rectangular, circular).
  • Combined footing — two or more columns on one footing (rectangular or trapezoidal).
  • Strap footing — two isolated footings joined by a rigid beam.
  • Mat / raft foundation — a single slab under the whole building (weak soil / heavy loads).
  • Pile foundation with pile cap — deep foundation transferring load to firm strata.

4.2 DESIGN OF ISOLATED FOOTING (AXIAL)

  1. Plan area: A = (P + self-weight, ~10% of P)/SBC (service load). Choose L×B.
  2. Net upward pressure (factored): pu = 1.5P / (L×B).
  3. Bending moment at the column face: Mu = pu·B·(cantilever)²2, where cantilever = L − column size2. Find effective depth from Mu = 0.138fckBd², then Ast.
  4. One-way (wide-beam) shear at distance d from the column face: τv = VuBd ≤ τc.
  5. Two-way (punching) shear on a perimeter d2 from the column face: τv = Vubod ≤ ks·0.25√fck (ks = 0.5 + βc ≤ 1). Punching usually governs the depth.
  6. Check development length of bars and bearing stress at the column-footing interface.

4.3 ISOLATED FOOTING WITH MOMENT (ECCENTRIC LOAD)

When the column transmits a moment M (or the load is eccentric, e = MP), the soil pressure is non-uniform:

pmax/min = PA ± MZ = (PLB)(1 ± 6eL). For no tension (uplift), keep e ≤ L6 (load within the middle third). If e > L6, only part of the base is in contact and pmax = 2P3B·(L2 − e).

Design the critical section for the larger pressure; provide steel for the maximum bending moment and check both shear conditions using the varying pressure.

4.4 COMBINED FOOTING

Used when columns are close together or near a property line. The footing is proportioned so the centroid of the base coincides with the resultant of the column loads, giving uniform pressure. Shape is rectangular (loads similar) or trapezoidal (loads unequal with a space constraint). It is analysed as a beam spanning between the columns (longitudinal bending, with the soil pressure acting upward) and as a slab in the transverse direction under each column.

4.5 MAT (RAFT) FOUNDATION

A mat covers the entire building footprint and is used when: (i) the SBC is low, (ii) column loads are heavy and footings would overlap (> ~50% of area), or (iii) differential settlement must be reduced. Design procedure:

  1. Check net upward pressure = Σloads/area ≤ SBC.
  2. Analyse by the conventional (rigid) method — treat as an inverted floor loaded by soil pressure, divide into strips, find moments and shears — or by the flexible (beam-on-elastic-foundation) method.
  3. Design the slab for the resulting moments and check punching shear at each column.

4.6 PILE CAPS (INTRODUCTION)

A pile cap is a thick RC slab that ties a group of piles together and distributes the column load to them. It is designed by the truss (strut-and-tie) analogy or the bending-theory method, with checks for bending, one-way shear and punching shear around the column and around individual piles. Minimum edge distance from a pile to the cap edge is typically 100–150 mm.
