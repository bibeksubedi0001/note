Question 1(a): Characteristic Load and Design Load

Define the characteristic load and design load used in structural design. [2 marks]

Characteristic load, F: the load value with a 95% probability of not being exceeded during the life of the structure. For design under IS 456, the specified dead, imposed, wind and snow loads and the seismic forces from the relevant loading standards are adopted as characteristic loads.

Design load, Fd: the characteristic load multiplied by the partial safety factor appropriate to the nature of the loading and the limit state being checked:

Fd = γfF.

The factor is not always 1.5: use the applicable combination and limit state. An action already described as ultimate or factored in this paper must not be multiplied by 1.5 again.

Find it in the permitted code: IS 456:2000, Clause 36.2, printed p. 67 / PDF p. 68; Clause 36.3.2, printed p. 68 / PDF p. 69. Clause 36.4.1 and Table 18 on printed p. 68 / PDF p. 69 give the load-factor basis.

Question 1(b): Transverse Reinforcement for Shear and Torsion

A reinforced concrete (RC) rectangular beam has a cross-section of 300 mm × 500 mm. The beam is reinforced with 4 bars of 16 mm diameter on the tension side and 2 bars of 12 mm diameter on the compression side. If the section is subjected to an ultimate bending moment of 60 kN·m, an ultimate twisting moment of 30 kN·m, and an ultimate shear force of 80 kN, design the required transverse (shear) reinforcement. Assume an effective cover of 50 mm. Use M20 grade concrete and Fe 415 steel. [7 marks]

Method and detailing basis: use the equivalent-action and closed-hoop method in Bhavikatti, Chapter 6, §6.13 and Example 6.7 (printed pp. 128–131 / supplied PDF pp. 135–138), checked against IS 456 §41. Take the given 50 mm effective cover to the corner-bar centres at both faces and sides: b1 = 200 mm and d1 = 400 mm. These are longitudinal-bar centre distances, not the concrete section dimensions.

pt = 100Ast/(bd) = 0.596%.

τc = 0.48 + [(0.59574 − 0.50)/0.25](0.56 − 0.48) = 0.5106 N/mm².

Question 1(c): Critical Neutral-Axis Factor in WSM

Derive from first principles the expression for the critical neutral axis depth factor (k) for a rectangular singly reinforced section using the Working Stress Method (WSM). [3 marks]

At the critical or balanced working-stress section, the extreme concrete compressive stress and the tensile steel stress reach their respective permissible values, σcbc and σst, simultaneously. This is a permissible-stress condition, not a physical ultimate-failure state.

Let the effective depth be d, the neutral-axis depth x = kd, and m = Es/Ec be the modular ratio used by WSM. Plane sections remain plane, bond is perfect and the materials are treated as linear elastic at working loads. Hence the similar strain triangles give:

εc/εs = x/(d − x) = k/(1 − k).
εc = σcbc/Ec; εs = σst/Es.
Therefore, mσcbc/σst = k/(1 − k).
mσcbc(1 − k) = kσst.

k = mσcbc/(σst + mσcbc) = 1/[1 + σst/(mσcbc)].

For IS 456 WSM, use the code's effective modular ratio m = 280/(3σcbc), with stresses in N/mm². It incorporates long-term effects and should not be replaced by Es/(5000√fck) in this derivation. Force equilibrium additionally gives C = ½σcbcbx = Astσst, and the lever arm is z = d − x/3 = d(1 − k/3).

Find it in the permitted code: IS 456:2000, Annex B, §B-1.3(a)–(d), printed p. 80 / PDF p. 81, states the elastic assumptions and modular ratio; Tables 21 and 22, printed pp. 81–82 / PDF pp. 82–83, give permissible stresses. The algebraic derivation is from these assumptions; compare the Manual's critical-neutral-axis discussion, supplied PDF p. 7.

Question 2(a): Anchorage Bond, Flexural Bond and End Anchorage

Define Anchorage bond and Flexure bond and derive the equation Ld ≤ 1.3M1/V + Lo, where the symbols have their usual meaning. [4 marks]

Anchorage bond transfers the force in a reinforcing bar to the surrounding concrete over an embedded length, preventing withdrawal or end slip. Flexural bond transfers the change in longitudinal steel force caused by the variation of bending moment along a member. They describe different reasons for the same steel–concrete interface shear, not two independent strengths to add.

1. Development Length from Force Equilibrium

Force in one bar = (πφ²/4)σs.
Bond resistance over Ld = πφLdτbd.
Equating them: Ld = φσs/(4τbd).

2. Flexural-Bond Relation

For a short length dx with approximately constant internal lever arm z, M = Tz. Thus dT = dM/z = V dx/z. If u is the sum of the bar perimeters, bond equilibrium gives uτbdx = dT:

τb = V/(uz).
M1 = Asσsz; Ld = Asσs/(uτbd).
Therefore, Ld/(M1/V) = V/(uzτbd) = τb/τbd.

Consequently, the basic bond condition τb ≤ τbd corresponds to Ld ≤ M1/V. At a simple support or point of inflection, the available continuation/anchorage Lo is credited by the code, giving Ld ≤ M1/V + Lo. If a compressive support reaction confines the reinforcement ends, the code permits M1/V to be increased by 30%:

Ld ≤ 1.3M1/V + Lo, only when the reinforcement ends are confined by a compressive reaction.

The 1.3 coefficient is a code allowance, not a number derived from elastic equilibrium alone. Without that confinement, retain the coefficient 1.0. This check also limits the bar diameter, because Ld increases with φ.

  • Ld: development length corresponding to the design bar stress; σs = 0.87fy at full design strength in LSM.
  • M1: moment resistance with all reinforcement at the section assumed stressed to its design stress; it is not the zero applied moment at the support.
  • V: design shear force at the section.
  • Lo: at a simple support, the anchorage beyond the support centre plus equivalent anchorage of hooks or mechanical anchorage. At an inflection point, the credited Lo is limited to max(d, 12φ).

Find it in the permitted code: IS 456:2000, §26.2.1, printed p. 42 / PDF p. 43, for development length; §26.2.1.1 and §26.2.2.1, printed p. 43 / PDF p. 44, for bond stress and bends/hooks; §26.2.3.3(c), printed p. 44 / PDF p. 45, for the complete inequality, symbol definitions and the 30% allowance. The Manual's beam chapter also discusses this examination derivation on supplied PDF p. 77.

Question 3(a): Control of Deflection and Cracking

Explain control of Deflection and control of Cracking in RCC structure design. [2 marks]

Deflection control: choose adequate member depth and stiffness so that service-load deformation does not damage partitions or finishes or impair use. IS 456 limits final deflection, including creep, shrinkage and temperature effects, normally to span/250. Deflection occurring after partitions/finishes are installed is normally limited to the smaller of span/350 and 20 mm. For ordinary members, satisfy the code's modified span/effective-depth limits: basic ratios 7 for cantilevers, 20 for simply supported members and 26 for continuous members, modified for reinforcement and other applicable conditions. Otherwise calculate deflection using Annex C.

Crack control: distribute sufficient reinforcement with suitable bar diameters, spacing and cover; detail anchorage, restraint and construction joints appropriately, and ensure proper placing and curing. Many well-distributed narrow cracks are preferable to a few wide cracks. The code's general surface-width limits are 0.3 mm where cracking is not harmful, 0.2 mm where exposure/moisture or contact with soil makes tensile cracking harmful, and 0.1 mm for particularly aggressive environments. When a specific width must be demonstrated, calculate it using Annex F rather than assuming that strength alone controls it.

Find it in the permitted code: IS 456:2000, §23.2 and §23.2.1, printed p. 37 / PDF p. 38, Figures 4–6 on printed pp. 38–39 / PDF pp. 39–40; §35.3.1 and §35.3.2, printed p. 67 / PDF p. 68; §§42–43, printed pp. 75–76 / PDF pp. 76–77. The crack-width formula is in Annex F, printed p. 95 / PDF p. 96.

Question 4(a): Under-Reinforced Sections

Define an under-reinforced section. Why is it preferred in LSM design? [2 marks]

An under-reinforced flexural section has less tensile reinforcement than the limiting amount for its geometry, materials and compression reinforcement. In a singly reinforced rectangular beam this corresponds to xu < xu,max. The tension steel reaches substantial inelastic strain before the concrete compression zone reaches its crushing limit.

It is preferred because it gives ductile deformation and visible cracking/deflection before collapse, permits rotation and moment redistribution when properly detailed, and can dissipate energy. An over-reinforced section can fail by comparatively sudden concrete crushing before sufficient tensile-steel strain develops. Under-reinforcement alone does not prevent brittle shear, bond or joint failure; those modes must be designed and detailed separately.

IS 456 requires εst at failure ≥ 0.002 + fy/(1.15Es).
With εcu = 0.0035 and a linear strain diagram:
xu,max/d = 0.0035/[0.0035 + 0.002 + fy/(1.15Es)].
Code values: Fe250, 0.53; Fe415, 0.48; Fe500, 0.46.

Question 5(a): Importance of Ductile Detailing

Describe the importance of ductile detailing in RC structures with neat sketches showing the ductile detailing requirements for beams and columns. [4 marks]

Ductile detailing enables an RC member and its connections to sustain repeated inelastic deformation under earthquake loading while retaining useful strength and gravity-load capacity. It confines the concrete core, restrains buckling of longitudinal bars, maintains anchorage, and prevents brittle shear, splice and joint failures from preceding the intended flexural yielding. Closely spaced hoops are therefore not merely nominal cage-holding steel.

Beam Requirements

  • Geometry and longitudinal bars: width at least 200 mm, width/depth ratio preferably greater than 0.3, and depth not greater than one-quarter of clear span, subject to the code's stated exception. Provide at least two 12 mm bars each at top and bottom. Minimum longitudinal ratio ρmin = 0.24√fck/fy; maximum on a face ρmax = 0.025.
  • Moment reversal: bottom longitudinal steel at a joint face is at least half the top steel there. Steel on either face at any section is at least one-quarter of the maximum support top steel. Fully anchor beam bars in joints; at an exterior joint the prescribed anchorage is Ld,tension + 10db minus the 90°-bend allowance.
  • Shear links: vertical closed links, diameter at least 8 mm. Within 2d of each joint face, spacing s ≤ min(d/4, 8db,min, 100 mm); place the first link within 50 mm of the joint face. Elsewhere s ≤ d/2. Provide similar close links on either side of any other likely yielding section.
  • Capacity design: design for the larger of analysis shear and the equilibrium shear associated with end plastic-hinge capacities plus gravity load. The supplied 2016 code excludes concrete, inclined links and bent-up bars from the seismic beam shear-capacity calculation.
  • Splices and hooks: no lap in the joint, within 2d of its face, or within the specified potential-yielding regions; no more than 50% of a face's bars spliced at one section; links over laps at no more than 150 mm. Use the code's 135° hook and tail of at least max(6dlink, 65 mm), embedded in the confined core.

Column and Joint Requirements

  • Geometry and hierarchy: minimum column dimension is the larger of 300 mm and 20 times the largest beam-bar diameter passing through or anchoring in the joint; rectangular aspect ratio at least 0.45. Apply the strong-column/weak-beam check: the sum of column nominal moment strengths at a joint is at least 1.4 times that of the beams, with the specific code exceptions. Under the relevant seismic load combinations the factored axial stress limit in §7.1 is 0.40fck.
  • Hoops and cross-ties: closed hoops with 135° hooked ends and tails at least max(6dlink, 65 mm). Minimum link diameter is 8 mm for longitudinal bars up to 32 mm, and 10 mm for larger bars. Parallel link legs are at most 300 mm apart; add cross-ties or overlapping hoops where required, engaging the peripheral longitudinal bars.
  • Confinement length: at either end, lo ≥ max(larger section dimension, clear height/6, 450 mm). Within this length, sv ≤ min(one-quarter of the least section dimension, 6db,min, 100 mm), and calculate the required hoop area. Ordinary column-zone hoop spacing may not exceed half the least lateral dimension.
  • Splices: lap only in the central half of clear column height, outside joints and outside the prohibited 2d beam-face zones. Splice at most 50% at one section and use links at no more than 100 mm over the lap. Bars larger than 32 mm require mechanical splicing under the supplied code.
  • Special cases: extend confinement at least 300 mm into a footing. Provide full-height confinement for the conditions in §§8.3–8.4, including a contraflexure point outside the middle half or significant stiffness variation. Check joint shear and joint confinement as well as the members.

For rectangular links, the exact expression printed in the supplied IS 13920:2016, §8.1(c)(2), is:

Ash ≥ max{0.18svh(fck/fy)(Ag/Ak − 1), 0.05svh(fck/fy)}.

Here h is the longer outside dimension of a rectangular link, not exceeding 300 mm; it can be reduced by introducing the prescribed cross-ties. Ak is the confined-core area measured to the outer link dimensions and remains the overall core area when cross-ties are used. Ash is the area of the bar forming the link under this clause, not automatically the two-leg Asv used in ordinary beam shear design. The 0.05 term is not interchangeable with an older code's confinement expression.

Find it in the permitted code: IS 13920:2016, §6.1, printed p. 4 / PDF p. 6; §6.2 and Figures 2–3, printed p. 5 / PDF p. 7; §6.3 and Figures 4–6, printed pp. 6–7 / PDF pp. 8–9; §§7.1–7.2, printed pp. 7–8 / PDF pp. 9–10; §§7.3–7.4 and Figure 10, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 11–12; §8.1, printed pp. 11–12 / PDF pp. 13–14; §§8.2–8.4, printed p. 12 / PDF p. 14.
