A. Theory Answers (by chapter) concise

Ch 1 — Design methods & sections

  • WSM vs LSM / why LSM is better: WSM is deterministic, governed by elastic theory with a single factor of safety on the material and does not give a realistic factor of safety; LSM is probabilistic, uses separate partial safety factors for loads (γf) and materials (γm), checks both strength and serviceability, and gives more economical sections. (See Ch 1 §1.5–1.6.)
  • Characteristic strength/load: strength below which ≤ 5% of results fall; load with 95% probability of not being exceeded. Design: fd = f/γm, Fd = Fγf.
  • Balanced / under- / over-reinforced: defined by xu vs xu,max (= 0.53d/0.48d/0.46d for Fe250/415/500). Under-reinforced = ductile (preferred); over-reinforced = brittle (avoided). (Ch 1 §1.8, Ch 2 §2.4.)
  • Idealized stress-strain / modular ratio: m = 280/(3σcbc). (Ch 1 §1.7.)
  • Mechanical splices: parallel-thread couplers, taper-cut-thread couplers, cold-swaged sleeve with threaded end.

Ch 5/6 — Shear, torsion, bond, serviceability

  • Behaviour of concrete under shear / bent-up bars: diagonal tension cracks form; stirrups and bent-up bars carry the shear beyond τc. (Ch 2 §2.9.)
  • Anchorage vs flexural bond: anchorage bond resists pull-out along the embedded length; flexural (local) bond arises from the rate of change of bar force along the span. Code check Ld ≤ 1.3M1/Vu + Lo. (Ch 2 §2.11.)
  • Deflection & crack control: limit span/depth ratio (deflection); limit bar spacing/diameter and provide minimum steel (cracking); IS 456 surface crack-width formula. (Ch 5, IS 456 Annex.)
  • Splicing: required to join bars where lengths are limited; lap, welded or mechanical; stagger laps, avoid at high-stress zones.

Ch 3/4/7–11 — Columns, footings, slabs, detailing, EQ

  • Interaction diagram / short vs long column: see Ch 3 §3.4, §3.7.
  • Mat foundation / why no shear steel in footings: footings are kept thick enough that concrete alone carries the shear (economical, avoids congestion). (Ch 4.)
  • Ductility & ductile detailing (beam/column/joint): ability to deform inelastically without collapse; IS 13920 confining hoops, strong-column/weak-beam, joint confinement. (Ch 6 §6.3, §6.5.)
  • Philosophy of EQ design: no damage in minor, repairable in moderate, no collapse in major shaking. (Ch 6 §6.2.)

B1. WSM — Moment of Resistance 2071 Shrawan, 2073 Shrawan, 2075 Chaitra, 2081 Bhadra

Beam 250 mm × 300 mm, 3−25 mm φ tension bars; σcbc = 7, σst = 140 N/mm².

  1. Ast = 3×(π/4)×25² = 1471.9 mm²; m = 280/(3×7) = 13.33.
  2. Critical NA: σcbc/(σst/m) = Xc/(d−Xc) ⇒ Xc = 120 mm.
  3. Actual NA (b·x·x/2 = m·Ast(d−x)): 125x² + 19620x − 5.886×106 = 0 ⇒ x = 152.3 mm.
  4. x > Xc ⇒ over-reinforced. MOR = b·x·(σcbc/2)(d−x/3) = 250×152.3×3.5×(300−50.8) = 33.21 kN-m.

If x < Xc (under-reinforced), instead use MOR = Astσst(d − x/3).

B2. WSM — Design of a Beam 2071 Chaitra

Design a 300 × 450 mm section carrying 30 kN/m over 3.6 m; M20, mild steel.

  1. d = 450 − 50 = 400 mm; σcbc = 7, σst = 140 N/mm².
  2. M = wL²/8 = 30×3.6²/8 = 48.6 kN-m.
  3. ½σcbcb·x(d−x/3) = M ⇒ 350x² − 420000x + 48.6×106 = 0 ⇒ x = 129.74 mm.
  4. Ast = M/[σst(d−x/3)] = 48.6×106/(140×356.75) = 973 mm² ⇒ 4−20 mm φ bars.

B3. LSM — MOR of a Singly-Reinforced Beam 2073 Shrawan, 2074/75/81

b = 250, d = 360 mm, M20, Fe415, 3−16 mm φ bars.

  1. Ast = 602.88 mm²; xu = 0.87fyAst/(0.36fckb) = 120.93 mm.
  2. xu,max = 0.48×360 = 172.8 mm ⇒ xu < xu,max (under-reinforced).
  3. Mu = 0.87fyAstd[1 − Astfy/(bd fck)] = 67.47 kN-m.

B4. LSM — Design of a Beam in Flexure (Doubly-Reinforced + Deflection) 2072 Chaitra

SS beam, effective span 5.5 m, overall 230 × 550 mm, superimposed load 50 kN/m (excl. self-weight); design for LS collapse in flexure and check deflection. Fe415, mild exposure, effective cover 50 mm. (worked exactly as in the manual)

  1. d = 550 − 50 = 500 mm; assume M20. Self-weight = 25×0.23×0.55 = 3.16 kN/m → total = 53.16 kN/m → wu = 1.5×53.16 = 79.74 kN/m.
  2. Mu = wul²/8 = 79.74×5.5²/8 = 301.53 kN-m.
  3. xu,max = 0.48×500 = 240 mm; Mu,lim = 0.36fckb·xu,max(d−0.42xu,max) = 158.66 kN-m. Mu > Mu,lim ⇒ doubly-reinforced.
  4. Ast1 = Mu,lim/[0.87fy(d−0.42xu,max)] = 1100.79 mm²; Mu2 = 142.87 kN-m; Ast2 = Mu2/[0.87fy(d−d′)] = 879.36 mm². Ast = 1980.15 mm² → 5−25 mm φ (2453 mm²).
  5. Compression steel: εsc = 0.0035(xu,max−d′)/xu,max = 0.00276 → fsc = 351.8 (SP-16), fcc = 0.446fck = 8.92. Asc = Mu2/[(fsc−fcc)(d−d′)] = 925.96 mm² → 3−20 mm φ (942 mm²).
  6. Deflection: fs = 0.58fy(Ast,req/Ast,prov) = 194.3 N/mm²; pt = 2.13 → k2 = 0.98; pc = 0.82 → k3 = 1.21. (L/d)max = 1×0.98×1.21×20 = 23.7 > (L/d)prov = 5500/500 = 11 ⇒ safe in deflection.

Singly-reinforced case: if Mu ≤ Mu,lim = 0.138fckbd², solve Mu = 0.87fyAstd[1 − Astfy/(bd fck)] directly for a single layer of tension steel.

B5. Shear Reinforcement Design 2075 Chaitra, 2078/79/81

Beam 300 × 500 mm (d = 450), Vu = 200 kN, M20, Fe415, Ast = 4−20 mm.

  1. τv = Vu/(bd) = 200000/(300×450) = 1.48 N/mm².
  2. pt = 100×1257/(300×450) = 0.93% ⇒ τc ≈ 0.60 N/mm² (IS 456 Table 19); τc,max = 2.8 > τv OK.
  3. τc < τv ⇒ design stirrups. Vus = Vu − τcbd = 200000 − 81000 = 119000 N.
  4. 2-legged 8 mm stirrups: Asv = 100.5 mm². sv = 0.87fyAsvd/Vus = 361×100.5×450/119000 = 137 mm.
  5. Provide 8 mm 2-legged stirrups @ 135 mm c/c (≤ 0.75d = 337 and 300 mm).

Variant — bent-up bars (2070 Chaitra, worked as in the manual): beam d = 450, b = 250, Vu = 125 kN, M20, 4−20 mm TOR with 2 bars bent up at 30°.

  1. At the support (2 bars remain): Ast = 2×(π/4)×20² = 628 mm²; pt = 0.56% → τc = 0.50 N/mm²; τv = 125000/(250×450) = 1.11 N/mm².
  2. Shear resistance of the two bent-up bars = 2×0.87fyAsvsin30° = 2×113.37 = 226.7 kN.
  3. V′us = Vu − τcbd = 125 − 56.25 = 68.75 kN. IS 456 caps the bent-up contribution at 50% ⇒ bent-up bars carry 34.375 kN and stirrups carry the other 34.375 kN.
  4. Stirrups: sv = 0.87fyAsvd/Vus = 474.9 mm, but ≤ 0.75d = 337.5 and 300 ⇒ provide 8 mm 2-legged @ 300 mm c/c.

B6. Development Length 2076/79/81/82 (derivation)

Ld = φ·σs/(4τbd). For a 20 mm Fe415 bar in M20:

  1. σs = 0.87×415 = 361 N/mm²; τbd = 1.2 (M20) × 1.6 (deformed) = 1.92 N/mm².
  2. Ld = 20×361/(4×1.92) = 940 mm ≈ 47φ.
  3. At a simply supported end also check Ld ≤ 1.3M1/Vu + Lo.

B7. Column with Biaxial Bending 2072/74/75/76/78 …

Method (SP-16): given Pu, Mux, Muy, size b×D, grades.

  1. Assume steel p = 100Asc/(bD) (start ~1–2%); pick d′/D.
  2. From SP-16 charts read the uniaxial capacities Mux1, Muy1 for the given Pu/(fckbD).
  3. Compute Puz = 0.45fckAc + 0.75fyAsc → αn (1.0 at Pu/Puz = 0.2, 2.0 at 0.8).
  4. Check (Mux/Mux1)αn + (Muy/Muy1)αn ≤ 1.0. Increase steel and repeat until satisfied; detail bars and ties. (Ch 3 §3.6.)

B8. Isolated Footing 2073/74/76/78/80/81/82

Column 400 × 400 mm, service P = 1200 kN, SBC = 200 kN/m², M20/Fe415.

  1. Area = 1.1×1200/200 = 6.6 m² → 2.6 m × 2.6 m.
  2. Net factored pressure pu = 1.5×1200/6.76 = 266.3 kN/m².
  3. Cantilever a = (2600 − 400)/2 = 1100 mm; Mu = pu·B·a²/2 = 266.3×2.6×1.1²/2 = 418.9 kN-m.
  4. Depth for flexure d = √(Mu/(0.138fckB)) = 242 mm; punching shear (perimeter d/2, τv ≤ 0.25√fck = 1.12) governs → take D ≈ 550, d ≈ 500 mm.
  5. Find Ast from Mu (per m width), check one-way shear at d and development length. (Ch 4 §4.2.)

B9. Two-Way Slab 2073/75/78/79/80/81/82

Method (IS 456 Annex D): restrained panel ly/lx ≤ 2.

  1. Assume depth from L/d (short span, basic 26× for continuous × modification); compute wu = 1.5(DL + LL + FF).
  2. Read moment coefficients αx, αy (Table 26) for the panel type & aspect ratio.
  3. Mx = αxwulx², My = αywulx² (mid-span & support).
  4. Design steel both directions (short-span bars in the lower layer); provide corner torsion reinforcement = ¾ of mid-span steel over lx/5 where corners are held down.
  5. Check shear and deflection. (Ch 5 §5.3.)
