4.1 PROPOSAL WRITING AND REPORTING

LIKELY / PRACTICE QUESTIONS:

  1. What is a technical proposal? Describe the essential components of an engineering project proposal. [4]
  2. Differentiate between a solicited and an unsolicited proposal. [2]
  3. List and briefly explain the different types of engineering report. [4]
  4. Write short notes on: Structure of a feasibility report. [2]
Proposal
A structured written document that offers a plan, service or solution to a client or funding agency, seeking their approval and resources. It answers what will be done, why, how, by whom, when and at what cost.

Types of Proposal

  • Solicited — written in response to a client’s Request for Proposal (RFP) or Terms of Reference.
  • Unsolicited — initiated by the proposer to introduce an idea the client has not requested.
  • Internal vs external — within one’s own organization or to an outside party.

Components of a Technical Proposal

Title page → executive summary → introduction & problem statement → objectives & scope → methodology / technical approach → work plan & schedule → team & organization → budget/cost estimate → expected outcomes → conclusion & appendices.

4.2 PROFESSIONAL CORRESPONDENCE

LIKELY / PRACTICE QUESTIONS:

  1. What is professional correspondence? Explain the essential features of an effective business letter. [4]
  2. Write short notes on: Memo vs email in professional communication. [2]
  3. Draft a formal letter to a client requesting an extension of the project deadline. [4]
Professional Correspondence
The exchange of formal written communication — letters, memos, emails, notices and minutes — between an engineer/organization and clients, contractors, authorities or colleagues, for official record and action.

Common Forms

FormUse
Business letterFormal external communication (offers, requests, complaints, notices).
MemoBrief internal communication within an organization.
EmailFast, semi-formal communication with a written trail.
MinutesOfficial record of decisions taken in a meeting.
Notice / circularAnnouncement to many recipients at once.

The 7 C’s of Effective Correspondence

Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete and Courteous. A professional letter has a clear structure: sender’s address and date, recipient’s address, salutation, subject line, body (opening → details → action requested), complimentary close and signature.

Always keep tone formal and respectful, state the purpose early, keep one subject per letter, proofread for accuracy, and retain a copy for the record. Poor correspondence causes disputes; clear correspondence prevents them.

4.3 COMMUNICATION, TEAMWORK AND LEADERSHIP 2079 2078

MAPPED / PRACTICE QUESTIONS:

  1. Discuss the role of the engineer as a facilitator, including communication and leadership, in development activities. [4] (mapped from TES 2079 Shrawan, Q2)
  2. What are the barriers to effective communication? How can they be overcome? [4]
  3. Explain the qualities of a good leader in an engineering team. [4]

Communication

Communication is the two-way exchange of information, ideas and feelings that produces shared understanding. It may be verbal, non-verbal, written or visual, and flows downward, upward, horizontally or diagonally in an organization.

Barriers: language differences, non-verbal misreading, high-context vs low-context cultural styles, noise, information overload and status gaps. Overcoming them: use simple clear language, confirm understanding (feedback loop), active listening, choose the right medium and be culturally sensitive.

Teamwork

A team is a group working interdependently toward a shared goal. Effective teams share clear goals, defined roles, mutual trust, open communication, complementary skills and accountability. Engineering mega-projects — from ancient irrigation works to modern hydropower — have always depended on coordinated teamwork.

Leadership

Leadership is the ability to influence and guide a team toward goals. As a facilitator, the engineer serves as a technical expert, communication hub, meeting leader, conflict resolver and capacity builder — keeping a neutral view and making processes easier.

Leadership styles
  • Autocratic (leader decides)
  • Democratic (participative)
  • Laissez-faire (hands-off)
  • Transformational (inspires change)
Qualities of a good leader
  • Vision and integrity
  • Communication and empathy
  • Decisiveness and accountability
  • Ability to motivate and delegate

Continuing Professional Development (CPD)
The systematic maintenance, improvement and broadening of an engineer’s knowledge, skills and competence throughout their working life, keeping pace with advancing technology and evolving standards.

Why CPD Matters

  • Keeps skills current with new technology, codes and materials.
  • Maintains professional competence and public safety.
  • Supports career growth and licence renewal.
  • Builds credibility and lifelong employability.
