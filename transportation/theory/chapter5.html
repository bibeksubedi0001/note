5.1 Difference between maintenance and rehabilitation208020762075

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Describe the term maintenance, rehabilitation and reconstruction. Describe the typical types of rigid pavement failure with sketches where possible. [8] (2080 Bhadra, Q9)
    Solution

    Maintenance = routine/periodic works keeping a pavement serviceable (patching, crack sealing, seal coats). Rehabilitation = major restoration (overlays, recycling) of a deteriorated pavement to extend its life. Reconstruction = complete removal and rebuilding when it is beyond rehabilitation. Pavement evaluation: functional (roughness, skid resistance, PSI) and structural (deflection - Benkelman beam / FWD) methods.

    Typical rigid-pavement failures: cracking (corner, edge, transverse, longitudinal, shrinkage), joint failures (spalling, faulting, pumping), scaling/durability failure and blow-ups; caused by overloading, temperature warping, loss of subgrade support (pumping) and poor joints/materials.

  2. What are the most common failures of flexible pavement? Describe the deflection approach for the design of overlay for flexible pavement: [8] (2080 Baishakh, Q9)
    Solution

    Failures of flexible pavement (causes & remedies): alligator/fatigue cracking (overloading, weak subgrade → overlay/reconstruction); rutting (permanent deformation in wheel paths → overlay); corrugation/shoving (unstable mix → remove & relay); potholes (water + traffic → patching); raveling (loss of aggregate → seal coat); bleeding (excess bitumen → blot with sand); reflection/temperature cracking (→ crack sealing). Main causes: overloading, poor drainage, weak subgrade, poor materials/construction and ageing.

    Overlay design by the Benkelman-beam deflection method (IRC-81): (1) select representative points along the stretch; (2) measure the rebound deflection under the standard dual-wheel truck (4085 kg, 5.6 kg/cm2); (3) record pavement temperature & moisture; (4) apply temperature correction (to 35°C) and seasonal/moisture correction (here factor 1.3); (5) compute the characteristic deflection Dc = mean + 2σ; (6) determine the design traffic (CVPD & growth); (7) read the required overlay thickness from the IRC-81 design chart for Dc and the traffic.

  3. Differentiate between Repair and Rehabilitation [8] (2076 Ashwin, Q9)
    Solution

    Repair = localized corrective work fixing specific defects (patching potholes, sealing cracks) to restore serviceability - reactive, local, short-term. Rehabilitation = major, planned works (overlay, recycling, partial reconstruction) that structurally strengthen a deteriorated pavement and extend its life - planned, extensive, long-term.

  4. Describe briefly maintenance, rehabilitation and reconstruction. Describe the methods of pavement evaluation. (2075 Ashwin, Q8)
    Solution

    Cement Concrete Pavement construction: (1) prepare & compact subgrade/sub-base; (2) place side forms and a separation membrane; (3) position dowel & tie bars at joints; (4) batch, mix and place concrete; (5) consolidate (vibrate) and finish to level & texture; (6) form/cut joints (expansion, contraction); (7) moist-cure for the specified period; (8) seal joints and open after adequate strength. Quality control: workability (slump), flexural strength (beam), thickness & surface tolerance.

    Maintenance = routine/periodic works keeping a pavement serviceable (patching, crack sealing, seal coats). Rehabilitation = major restoration (overlays, recycling) of a deteriorated pavement to extend its life. Reconstruction = complete removal and rebuilding when it is beyond rehabilitation. Pavement evaluation: functional (roughness, skid resistance, PSI) and structural (deflection - Benkelman beam / FWD) methods.

5.8 IRC method of overlay design for flexible pavement20822081

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Benkelman beam deflection studies were carried out on 15 selected points on stretch of flexible pavement during summer season using a dual wheel load of 4085 kg, 5.6 kg/cm² pressure. The deflection values obtained in mm after making the necessary lag corrections are given below. If the present traffic consists of 750 CVPD, determine the thickness of bituminous overlay required, if the pavement temperature during the test was 30°C and the correction factor for subsequent increase in subgrade moisture content is 1.3. Assume annual rate of growth of traffic as 7.5%. [4+4] (2082 Bhadra, Q9)
    Solution

    Overlay design by the Benkelman-beam deflection method (IRC-81): (1) select representative points along the stretch; (2) measure the rebound deflection under the standard dual-wheel truck (4085 kg, 5.6 kg/cm2); (3) record pavement temperature & moisture; (4) apply temperature correction (to 35°C) and seasonal/moisture correction (here factor 1.3); (5) compute the characteristic deflection Dc = mean + 2σ; (6) determine the design traffic (CVPD & growth); (7) read the required overlay thickness from the IRC-81 design chart for Dc and the traffic.

    For this problem: corrected deflection = (mean measured) × temperature correction × 1.3 (moisture); Dc = x̄ + 2σ; read the overlay thickness from the IRC-81 chart for 750 CVPD with 7.5% growth over the design life.

    The individual deflection readings are not in the source paper - substitute them to get x̄, σ and the chart thickness.
