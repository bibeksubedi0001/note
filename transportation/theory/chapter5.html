    General causes can be grouped as: (i) failure of the subgrade — inadequate stability, excessive moisture, excessive stress, poor compaction; (ii) failure of the base or sub-base — inadequate stability or strength, loss of binding action, loss of base material, inferior materials, lack of lateral confinement; and (iii) failure of the wearing course — improper mix design, poor quality control, volatilisation and oxidation of the bitumen.

    (b) Deflection approach for the design of an overlay

    Deflection (Benkelman beam) approach of overlay design — IRC:81

    1. Principle. The structural strength of an existing flexible pavement is judged from the rebound deflection of its surface under a standard rear axle of 8.2 t (dual wheels, 5.6 kg/cm² tyre pressure). A larger deflection means a weaker pavement/subgrade; the overlay must be thick enough to bring the deflection down to a tolerable value.
    2. Field measurement. The Benkelman beam probe is inserted between the dual wheels; the initial dial reading D0 is taken, the truck is driven forward 2.7 m (intermediate reading Di) and then 9 m away (final reading Df). At least 10 points per section per lane, with a minimum of 20 points per km. If the highest or lowest value differs from the mean by more than one-third of the mean, extra readings are taken 25 m either side.
    3. Rebound deflection of each point (readings in divisions of 0.01 mm):
      if (Di − Df) ≤ 0.025 mm : D = 2(D0 − Df) × 0.01 mm if (Di − Df) > 0.025 mm : D = [2(D0 − Df) + 2×2.91(Di − Df)] × 0.01 mm
    4. Other data — pavement temperature at hourly intervals, field moisture content of the subgrade, subgrade soil classification, average annual rainfall, traffic volume, growth rate and axle-load (VDF) data.
    5. Characteristic deflection Dc = D̄ + s for roads with fewer than 1500 commercial vehicles per day, and D̄ + 2s for National and State Highways (D̄ = mean, s = standard deviation of the individual deflections).
    6. Corrections — (a) temperature: +0.01 mm per °C when the pavement temperature during the test is below 35°C and −0.01 mm per °C when it is above 35°C; (b) seasonal / moisture: a correction factor depending on the subgrade soil type (sandy-gravelly, clayey with PI < 15, clayey with PI > 15), the field moisture content at the time of the test and the average annual rainfall (< or > 1300 mm).
    7. Design traffic in million standard axles:
      Ns = 365 × A × [((1+r)ⁿ − 1)/r] × LDF × VDF
    8. Overlay thickness is read from the IRC:81 design chart (corrected characteristic deflection on the x-axis, cumulative standard axles as the family of curves) which gives the thickness of bituminous macadam. If other materials are used:
      1 cm BM ≡ 1.5 cm WBM / WMM / BUSG 1 cm BM ≡ 0.7 cm DBM / AC / SDBC

    Lecture 24 §5.3 and §5.8 (IRC:81); Manual.pdf Ch.4 §4.4 and §4.6.

  4. Describe briefly maintenance, rehabilitation and reconstruction. Describe the methods of pavement evaluation. (2075 Ashwin, Q9)
    Solution

    (a) Maintenance, rehabilitation and reconstruction

    TermMeaningTypical works
    MaintenanceWork carried out to preserve a pavement in its serviceable condition and to keep it as near as practicable to its original state. It does not add structural strength.Pot-hole patching, crack sealing, surface/seal coats, shoulder and drain cleaning, restoring camber, snow/silt removal
    RehabilitationMajor work that restores and increases the structural strength and riding quality of a pavement that has deteriorated but is not beyond repair.Strengthening overlays, recycling of the existing layers, partial or full-depth reconstruction of the surfacing, drainage improvement
    ReconstructionComplete removal of the existing pavement and rebuilding of all the layers when the pavement has failed structurally and rehabilitation is no longer economical.Removal of the old crust, re-preparation of the subgrade and construction of a new pavement

    On the pavement-condition-versus-time curve, maintenance keeps the condition in the "good" band, rehabilitation lifts a "fair/poor" pavement back up, and reconstruction is required once the pavement has reached the "fail" level.

    (b) Methods of pavement evaluation

    Pavement evaluation = assessing the existing condition of a pavement so that maintenance and strengthening works can be planned in time. It covers the subgrade support, the pavement composition and thickness, the present traffic loading and the environment, and is carried out in two ways:

    1. Structural evaluation — is the pavement structurally capable of carrying the design traffic?

    • Measured through surface deflection (Benkelman beam under a standard 8.2 t axle, falling weight deflectometer), and through stresses/strains at critical points.
    • Layer thicknesses (test pits) and material properties (CBR, resilient modulus) are also determined.
    • The measured deflection is used directly to design the strengthening overlay.

    2. Functional (surface condition) evaluation — is the ride quality acceptable?

    • Present Serviceability Index (PSI) — AASHTO index (0-5) from measured roughness, rut depth, cracking and patching.
    • Surface Distress Index (SDI) — a 6-level rating (0 = no visible defect, 5 = maximum defects) obtained from a visual distress survey; used to plan periodic maintenance.
    • International Roughness Index (IRI) — measured with a profilometer / bump integrator; quantifies the irregularity of the longitudinal profile.
    • Skid resistance — portable skid-resistance tester, sand-patch texture depth.

    Lecture 24 §5.1 and §5.6; Manual.pdf Ch.4 §4.1 and §4.3.

Highway Maintenance & Rehabilitation

  • Highway Maintenance is preserving and keeping the serviceable conditions of highway as normal as possible and as best as practicable. e.g. sealing of cracks, filling pot holes etc.
  • Highway Rehabilitation is improving the structural strength of the pavement. e.g. overlay, replacement of surfacing etc.

5.2 Classification of road maintenance2078

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Describe pavement maintenance and its types with examples. [8] (2078 Bhadra, Q4)
    Solution

    Pavement maintenance is the work carried out on a pavement to keep it in a serviceable condition as near as practicable to its original state, so that traffic can move safely, comfortably and economically, and so that the asset is preserved and the deterioration curve is flattened.

    Classification of road maintenance activities

    TypeFrequency / whenExamples
    Routine maintenanceContinuous / throughout the year, small localised worksCleaning of side drains, culverts and catch pits; cutting grass and bushes; repair of road furniture and signs; minor pot-hole patching; crack sealing
    Recurrent maintenanceSeveral times a year, depending on traffic and rainfallGrading/reshaping of gravel and earthen surfaces, restoring camber, gravel re-spreading, vegetation control, minor slips
    Periodic maintenanceAt intervals of several years, planned and budgetedResealing, surface dressing, fog/slurry seal, regravelling, thin overlays, renewal of markings
    Emergency (special) maintenanceUnpredictable — after a natural eventClearing landslides and debris, repair of washed-out sections, flood/earthquake damage, restoring cut lines
    Road-side maintenanceAlong with the aboveShoulders, side slopes, bio-engineering works, drainage structures and road furniture

    Why it matters: timely routine and periodic maintenance costs only a small fraction of the cost of rehabilitation or reconstruction; once a pavement is allowed to fall into the "poor" band the repair cost rises very steeply.

    Lecture 24 §5.2 (classification of road maintenance); Manual.pdf Ch.4 §4.1, book p.231.

Classification of Maintenance Activities

Highway maintenance can be classified as:

  • Road Maintenance
  • Roadside Maintenance

Road Maintenance

  • All maintenance works on the road way
  • Maintenance of all structures within road way (e.g. side ditches, culverts, causeways, bridges etc.)
  • Types:
    • Routine Maintenance
    • Recurrent Maintenance
    • Periodic Maintenance
    • Emergency Maintenance

Road Maintenance: Routine Maintenance

  • Maintenance operations of localize nature
  • Require continually on any road regardless of its engineering characteristics or traffic volume
    • Grass and bush cutting, tree pruning
    • Grading and reshaping of unpaved shoulders,
    • Clearing and cleaning of ditches and culverts,
    • Road sign, marking, lighting maintenance etc.
    • Winter maintenance (clearance of snow, ice)

Road Maintenance: Recurrent Maintenance

  • Maintenance operation of localized nature carried out at more or less regular interval of six months to two years with a frequency that depends on the traffic volume.
  • To be done by using minor equipment
    • Sealing cracks,
    • Local surface treatment,
    • Repair of depressions, holes and ruts, pot hole patching etc.

Road Maintenance: Periodic Maintenance

  • Maintenance operations of large extent required only at intervals of several years.
  • Frequency and type depend on the original surfacing, traffic, rainfall and climatic conditions
    • Resealing (surface treatment, slurry seal, thin overlay),
    • Re-gravelling of unpaved road
    • Restoration of road marking, drainage items etc.

Road Maintenance: Emergency Maintenance

  • Urgent emergency maintenance works needed for reopening of the road after slides, road wash outs etc.
  • Removal of debris or any other obstacles
  • Reinstatement of the pavement structures and drainage after rainy season
  • Strengthening/reconstruction of pavement
  • Protect road against falls of rock, flooding etc.

Road Side Maintenance

  • Concerns all maintenance works on structures and surface above and below the road having direct active and/or passive influence on the road. E.g. maintenance of culverts protection works, retaining walls, cut slopes, fill slopes, unstable natural slopes, river protection works and vegetation structures.

  2. Explain the maintenance of bituminous surfaces Describe the typical types of rigid pavement failures (2075 Chaitra, Q9)
    Solution

    (a) Maintenance of bituminous surfaces

    Maintenance of bituminous surfaces

    • Pot-hole patching — mark a rectangular area around the pot-hole, cut vertical faces to the full depth of the defective material, clean and dry the hole, apply a tack coat to the sides and bottom, fill with premix in layers and compact each layer, finishing slightly proud of the surface.
    • Crack sealing / filling — clean the crack with compressed air, fill fine cracks with a fog spray or emulsion slurry, and wide cracks with a hot sealant, then blind with sand.
    • Repair of ruts, corrugations and shoving — scarify or cold-mill the unstable surfacing, apply a tack coat, fill with a stable premix and roll; slight ruts may be filled with a levelling course followed by a seal.
    • Surface / seal coat, sand seal, slurry seal, fog seal — periodic treatments that waterproof a dry, ravelled or cracked surface and restore skid resistance.
    • Treating bleeding — blot the excess binder with hot sand or fine aggregate and roll; remove the surfacing where the bleeding is severe.
    • Edge repair and shoulder maintenance — make up the shoulder to the pavement level to restore lateral support and drainage.
    • Overlay — when the whole section is structurally deficient (see the Benkelman-beam deflection design).

    • Failure of the subgrade — inadequate stability, excessive moisture (poor drainage), excessive stress from overloading, and inadequate compaction. It shows up as undulations, rutting and depressions.

    • Failure of the wearing course — improper mix design (excess or deficient binder), inadequate quality control during construction, and volatilisation and oxidation (ageing) of the bitumen. It shows up as ravelling, bleeding, stripping, cracking and loss of texture.

Types of Road Failure and Its Causes

Road Failures:

  • Failures in Flexible Pavements
  • Failures in Rigid Pavements

Failures in Flexible Pavements

Flexible pavement failures in general appears as:

  • Excessive deformation/settlements of any layer
  • Excessive undulation or waves and corrugation
  • Lateral shoving of the pavement near the edge along the wheel path
  1. Failure of Subgrade: Causes:
  • Inadequate stability or strength
  • Excessive stress application
  • Inadequate compaction
  1. Failure of Subbase or Base: Causes:
  • Inadequate stability or strengths
  • Loss of binding action
  • Loss of base course materials
  • Inadequate wearing course
  • Use of inferior materials or inadequate quality control
  • Lack of lateral confinement for the granular base course
  1. Failure of Wearing Course: Causes:
  • Improper mix design
  • Inadequate quality control during construction
  • Volatilization and oxidation of bitumen

Typical Flexible Pavement Failures

a) Alligator (Map) Cracking:

Cause: Fatigue, localize weakness in base course

Repair: Because a structural failure is taking place, the only possible solution is a full-depth patch

b) Rutting: Channelized depressions along the wheel-path

Causes: Due to consolidation, lateral movement of any pavement layer, insufficient pavement thickness and compaction

Repair: overlay for minor, full depth removal and replacement with suitable material for severe one

c) Longitudinal Cracking: Cracks parallel to the pavement’s centerline.

Causes: Moisture infiltration, differential volume change, frost action, and/or poor joint construction.

Repair: Crack <1/2 inch can be sealed, severe cracks - remove the cracked pavement layer and replace it with an overlay.

d) Reflection Cracking:

Observed in a flexible overlay over a rigid pavement. Occur directly over the underlying rigid pavement joints or over cracks in rigid pavement

Causes: Movement of the rigid pavement slab due to thermal and moisture changes.

Repair: Crack sealing for cracks less than 1/2 inch, and removal of the cracked pavement layer followed by an overlay for severe one

e) Pot Holes:

Small, bowl-shaped depressions in the pavement surface that penetrate up to the base course.

Causes: Moisture infiltration and usually the end result of untreated alligator cracking.

Repair: Full depth replacement patch

f) Corrugations (Ripples) and Shoving

(Wave): Ripples or an abrupt wave across the pavement surface.

Cause: Usually by traffic action (braking and accelerating) combined with an unstable asphalt layer (excess/soft binder, poor compaction).

Repair: Small distortion – patching, large – overlay

g) Slippage Cracking Cause: Slipping occurs when the surface is not bound with the underlying layers or low strength surface mix. Slowly, it forms pot holes

Repair: Removal and replacement of affected area.

h) Shear Failure

Upheaval of pavement followed with depressions.

Cause: Excessive loads causing a shear failure in the subgrade, base course, or the surface

Repair: Removal and replacement of affected area.

i) Raveling

The separation of aggregate particles in a pavement. Usually, the fine aggregate wears away first. Soon larger particles are broken free.

Cause: Loss of bond between aggregate and bitumen

Repair: small raveling – patching, for large raveled area a thin hot-mix overlay

j) Frost Heaving

Localized heaving up of portion of pavement.

Cause: Frost action Repair: Small distortion – patching, large – overlay

k) Polished Aggregate Areas of pavement where the portion of aggregate extending above the asphalt binder is either very small or there are no rough or angular aggregate particles and have become smooth.

Cause: Abrasive action from repeated traffic applications, soft aggregate

Repair: Slurry seal or abrasion free overlay

Failures in Rigid Pavements

Formation of cracks is the main cause of failure. They fails mainly due to two factors:

  • Deficiency of pavement material:
    • Soft aggregate
    • Poor workmanship in joints
    • Poor joint filler and sealer material
    • Poor surface finish
    • Improper and insufficient curing
  • Structural inadequacy of pavement:
    • Inadequate pavement thickness
    • Inadequate sub grade supports and poor sub grade soil
    • Incorrect spacing of joints

Typical Rigid Pavement Failures

a) Scaling of Cement Concrete Deterioration and flaking of the concrete surface layer resulting exposure of aggregate.

Cause: Poor-quality concrete mix, high water-cement ratio, excessive vibration, improper finishing. Excess water or overworking can bring water and cement paste to the surface resulting weak surface.

Repair: Cement mortar repair, or Bituminous patch.

b) Shrinkage Crack

Hairline cracks formed during concrete setting and curing that are away from joints.

Cause: Inadequate reinforcement, improper or inadequate curing, high early age shrinkage

Repair: Moderate cracks are sealed, for severe cracks - slab replacement

c) Spalling of Joints

Breakdown of the slab edges, generally an indicator of advanced joint deterioration

Cause: Excessive stresses at the joint, low quality concrete, disintegration of the PCC from freeze-thaw

Repair: Partial or full depth patch

d) Warping Crack

Cause: Excessing warping stresses, inadequate joint design or inadequate reinforcement.

Repair: Hinge joints are provided with adequate reinforcement

e) Corner Crack/Break

A crack that intersects the PCC slab joints near the corner.

Cause: Load repetitions combined with a loss of support, poor load transfer across the joint, curling stresses and warping stresses.

Repair: Full depth Patch

f) Mud Pumping Movement of material underneath the slab or ejection of material from underneath the slab. Due to repeated occurrence, slab losses subgrade support.

Cause: Water accumulation due to a high water table, poor drainage, and cracks or poor joint seals that allow water to infiltrate

Repair: Full depth patch to remove any deteriorated slab area & stabilize subgrade.

g) Pop Out

Causes : Use of non durable aggregates susceptible to freezing/expansion, internal pressure caused by expansion of the affected aggregate

Repair: Removal of loose concrete and patch

h) Blow out

A localized upward slab movement and shattering at a joint or crack.

Cause: Insufficient room for slab expansion during hot weather

Repair: Full depth patch

i) Polished Aggregate Areas of pavement where there are no rough or angular aggregate particles, loss of skid resistance

Cause: Due to abrasion of soft aggregates

Repair: Abrasion free overlay

j) Faulting Difference in elevation at a joint or crack.

Cause: Settlement of pavement due to soft foundation, pumping or erosion of material, curling of slab edges due to temperature changes.

Repair: Dowel bar retrofit in an undoweled system, for severe- total reconstruction.

    Solution

    (a) Importance of road maintenance [2]

    • Preserves a very large public investment — timely maintenance costs a small fraction of rehabilitation or reconstruction.

    (b) Maintenance of bituminous pavement [6]

    Maintenance of bituminous surfaces

    • Pot-hole patching — mark a rectangular area around the pot-hole, cut vertical faces to the full depth of the defective material, clean and dry the hole, apply a tack coat to the sides and bottom, fill with premix in layers and compact each layer, finishing slightly proud of the surface.

    • Treating bleeding — blot the excess binder with hot sand or fine aggregate and roll; remove the surfacing where the bleeding is severe.
    • Edge repair and shoulder maintenance — make up the shoulder to the pavement level to restore lateral support and drainage.
    • Overlay — when the whole section is structurally deficient (see the Benkelman-beam deflection design).

5.6 Pavement evaluation method2076

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Define pavement evaluation. Explain the types of failure and their causes in flexible pavement. [8] (2076 Chaitra, Q9)
    Solution

    (a) Pavement evaluation (definition and methods)

    Pavement evaluation = assessing the existing condition of a pavement so that maintenance and strengthening works can be planned in time. It covers the subgrade support, the pavement composition and thickness, the present traffic loading and the environment, and is carried out in two ways:

    1. Structural evaluation — is the pavement structurally capable of carrying the design traffic?

    • Measured through surface deflection (Benkelman beam under a standard 8.2 t axle, falling weight deflectometer), and through stresses/strains at critical points.
    • Layer thicknesses (test pits) and material properties (CBR, resilient modulus) are also determined.
    • The measured deflection is used directly to design the strengthening overlay.

    2. Functional (surface condition) evaluation — is the ride quality acceptable?

    • Present Serviceability Index (PSI) — AASHTO index (0-5) from measured roughness, rut depth, cracking and patching.
    • Surface Distress Index (SDI) — a 6-level rating (0 = no visible defect, 5 = maximum defects) obtained from a visual distress survey; used to plan periodic maintenance.
    • International Roughness Index (IRI) — measured with a profilometer / bump integrator; quantifies the irregularity of the longitudinal profile.
    • Skid resistance — portable skid-resistance tester, sand-patch texture depth.

    (b) Types of failure in flexible pavement and their causes

    Common failures of flexible pavement, their causes and remedies

    FailureDescription & causeRemedy
    Alligator (map) crackingA series of interconnected cracks. Cause: fatigue under repeated loading, localised weakness in the base course, poor drainageStructural failure — the only solution is a full-depth patch
    RuttingChannelised depressions along the wheel path. Cause: consolidation and lateral movement of any pavement layer, insufficient thickness, inadequate compactionOverlay for minor rutting; full-depth removal and replacement for severe rutting
    Longitudinal crackingCracks parallel to the centre line. Cause: moisture infiltration, differential volume change, frost action, poor joint constructionCrack < 12 mm — seal; severe — remove the cracked layer and overlay
    Reflection crackingCracks in a flexible overlay directly over the joints/cracks of the old rigid pavement. Cause: movement of the underlying slab from thermal and moisture changeSeal cracks < 12 mm; otherwise remove the cracked layer and overlay (with a stress-absorbing membrane)
    Pot-holesBowl-shaped holes penetrating up to the base course. Cause: moisture infiltration, and the end result of untreated alligator crackingFull-depth replacement patch
    Corrugation and shovingTransverse ripples / a bulge of the surface. Cause: unstable mix (excess binder or fines), lack of aggregate interlock, braking and accelerating trafficRemove and replace the unstable surfacing; scarify and relay
    Ravelling / strippingProgressive loss of aggregate from the surface. Cause: insufficient binder, poor adhesion (stripping in the presence of water), laying in cold or wet weatherFog seal / surface dressing; remove and relay if deep
    BleedingA film of binder on the surface. Cause: excess bitumen in the mix or in the prime/tack coat, low air voids, high temperatureBlot with hot sand/aggregate and roll; for severe cases remove the surfacing
    Slippage crackingCrescent-shaped cracks. Cause: the surfacing is not bonded to the layer below (missing/poor tack coat) or a low-strength surface mixRemove the affected area and relay with a proper tack coat
    Edge cracking / failureCracks and break-up near the pavement edge. Cause: lack of lateral support from the shoulder, poor edge drainageRepair the shoulder, provide edge drainage, seal the crack or patch
    Polished aggregateLoss of surface texture and skid resistance. Cause: soft aggregate polished by trafficSurface dressing or a thin friction course

    General causes can be grouped as: (i) failure of the subgrade — inadequate stability, excessive moisture, excessive stress, poor compaction; (ii) failure of the base or sub-base — inadequate stability or strength, loss of binding action, loss of base material, inferior materials, lack of lateral confinement; and (iii) failure of the wearing course — improper mix design, poor quality control, volatilisation and oxidation of the bitumen.

Structural Evaluation of Pavements

  • Pavement may be evaluated by the raveling, ruts, patches , cracks and surface roughness

  • Road roughness is the irregularities in the pavement surface affecting user comfort and safety due to variations in horizontal, veridical, and transverse profiles. Profilometer is a measuring instrument used to quantify its roughness as IRI.

  2. Write down the process of overlay design of pavement by Benkelman beam Deflection Method. [8] (2081 Bhadra, Q9)
    Solution

    Deflection (Benkelman beam) approach of overlay design — IRC:81

    1. Principle. The structural strength of an existing flexible pavement is judged from the rebound deflection of its surface under a standard rear axle of 8.2 t (dual wheels, 5.6 kg/cm² tyre pressure). A larger deflection means a weaker pavement/subgrade; the overlay must be thick enough to bring the deflection down to a tolerable value.
    2. Field measurement. The Benkelman beam probe is inserted between the dual wheels; the initial dial reading D0 is taken, the truck is driven forward 2.7 m (intermediate reading Di) and then 9 m away (final reading Df). At least 10 points per section per lane, with a minimum of 20 points per km. If the highest or lowest value differs from the mean by more than one-third of the mean, extra readings are taken 25 m either side.
    3. Rebound deflection of each point (readings in divisions of 0.01 mm):
      if (Di − Df) ≤ 0.025 mm : D = 2(D0 − Df) × 0.01 mm if (Di − Df) > 0.025 mm : D = [2(D0 − Df) + 2×2.91(Di − Df)] × 0.01 mm
    4. Other data — pavement temperature at hourly intervals, field moisture content of the subgrade, subgrade soil classification, average annual rainfall, traffic volume, growth rate and axle-load (VDF) data.
    5. Characteristic deflection Dc = D̄ + s for roads with fewer than 1500 commercial vehicles per day, and D̄ + 2s for National and State Highways (D̄ = mean, s = standard deviation of the individual deflections).
    6. Corrections — (a) temperature: +0.01 mm per °C when the pavement temperature during the test is below 35°C and −0.01 mm per °C when it is above 35°C; (b) seasonal / moisture: a correction factor depending on the subgrade soil type (sandy-gravelly, clayey with PI < 15, clayey with PI > 15), the field moisture content at the time of the test and the average annual rainfall (< or > 1300 mm).
    7. Design traffic in million standard axles:
      Ns = 365 × A × [((1+r)ⁿ − 1)/r] × LDF × VDF
    8. Overlay thickness is read from the IRC:81 design chart (corrected characteristic deflection on the x-axis, cumulative standard axles as the family of curves) which gives the thickness of bituminous macadam. If other materials are used:
      1 cm BM ≡ 1.5 cm WBM / WMM / BUSG 1 cm BM ≡ 0.7 cm DBM / AC / SDBC

5.9 Concept of performance based maintenance

Performance Based Maintenance

  • In traditional maintenance contracts, road agency specifies the maintenance methods, materials, quantities, technologies, and time of work, and the contractor is paid based on the quantity of work or inputs used.
  • In performance-based maintenance contract (PBC), the road agency specifies the required condition or performance level of the road, and the contractor is paid based on whether the specified performance standards are achieved.
  • PBC in general is long-term agreement between the road agency and private contractor.
  • Encourages preventive and timely maintenance.
  • Transfers more responsibility and risk to the contractors. However, the higher risk can also provide opportunities for higher profit.
