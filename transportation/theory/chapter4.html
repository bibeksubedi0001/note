4.1 Activities and techniques used in road construction20752072

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. What are the various activities involved in road construction? Write the plants and equipment required for bituminous and cement concrete road constructions (2075 Chaitra, Q7)
    Solution

    Activities in road construction: setting-out/survey, site clearing, earthwork (cut & fill), subgrade preparation, sub-base & base construction, surfacing (bituminous/concrete), drainage & cross-drainage, and finishing (shoulders, markings, furniture).

    Plants for a bituminous road: hot-mix plant, bitumen distributor/sprayer, paver finisher, tippers, smooth & pneumatic rollers, chip spreader. For a CC road: batching & mixing plant, transit mixers, concrete paver/finisher, vibrators, joint-cutting machine, curing equipment.

  2. What are the various activities involved in road construction? Explain the construction procedure of otta seal (2072 Chaitra, Q7)
    Solution

    Activities in road construction: survey, clearing, earthwork, subgrade, sub-base/base, surfacing, drainage and finishing.

    Otta seal construction: a surface dressing using graded aggregate on a thick soft-bitumen film. Prepare/clean the base, spray a generous amount of soft/medium bitumen, spread graded natural gravel, roll heavily (pneumatic) so the binder works up through the aggregate, and roll/broom over a few days under traffic. Suited to low-volume roads.

    Solution

Materials: bitumen/emulsion and two sizes of cover aggregate.

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Describe the characteristics of mass haul diagram with neat sketch. Define shrinkage and swelling of earth. [6+2] (2082 Bhadra, Q8)
    Solution

    Mass Haul Diagram (MHD): a curve of cumulative (shrinkage/swell-corrected) earthwork volume plotted against chainage below the longitudinal profile. Characteristics: a rising curve = cut, a falling curve = fill; the difference of two ordinates = net volume between them; a horizontal balancing line cuts equal cut and fill (a balanced length); maxima/minima occur at cut→fill transition (grade) points; loops above the balancing line = haul left→right, below = right→left; the area between the curve and the balancing line = haul (volume × distance).

    Free haul = distance within which haul is paid for in the excavation rate (no extra cost). Over haul = haul beyond the free-haul distance (paid extra) = volume × (haul − free-haul distance). Economic haul distance = the limit beyond which it is cheaper to waste & borrow than to haul. Shrinkage = volume reduction when loose excavated soil is compacted in fill; swelling = volume increase when rock is excavated/loosened.

4.10 Cement concrete pavement and joints in cement concrete pavement20812075

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Differentiate between rolled concrete and normal concrete used in road construction. Describe the construction steps of cement concrete pavement with material and quality control. [8] (2081 Bhadra, Q8)
    Solution

    Rolled (roller-compacted) concrete is a zero-slump, dry mix compacted by vibratory rollers (no forms, dowels or finishing), whereas normal concrete pavement is a workable mix placed in forms, finished and cured with joints/reinforcement.

    Cement Concrete Pavement construction: (1) prepare & compact subgrade/sub-base; (2) place side forms and a separation membrane; (3) position dowel & tie bars at joints; (4) batch, mix and place concrete; (5) consolidate (vibrate) and finish to level & texture; (6) form/cut joints (expansion, contraction); (7) moist-cure for the specified period; (8) seal joints and open after adequate strength. Quality control: workability (slump), flexural strength (beam), thickness & surface tolerance.

  2. Describe the construction steps of cement concrete pavement. (2075 Ashwin, Q8)
    Solution

    Cement Concrete Pavement construction: (1) prepare & compact subgrade/sub-base; (2) place side forms and a separation membrane; (3) position dowel & tie bars at joints; (4) batch, mix and place concrete; (5) consolidate (vibrate) and finish to level & texture; (6) form/cut joints (expansion, contraction); (7) moist-cure for the specified period; (8) seal joints and open after adequate strength. Quality control: workability (slump), flexural strength (beam), thickness & surface tolerance.

    Maintenance = routine/periodic works keeping a pavement serviceable (patching, crack sealing, seal coats). Rehabilitation = major restoration (overlays, recycling) of a deteriorated pavement to extend its life. Reconstruction = complete removal and rebuilding when it is beyond rehabilitation. Pavement evaluation: functional (roughness, skid resistance, PSI) and structural (deflection - Benkelman beam / FWD) methods.
