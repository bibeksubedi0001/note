Traffic Volume

  • Number of vehicles that pass a point on a highway or a given lane or direction of a highway during a specified time interval.
  • A quantity measure of flow
  • ‘vehicles’ although often expressed as vehicles per unit time, Eg. veh/hr, veh/day

Traffic Volume Study

  • Volume study is a process of counting traffic volume on a highway facility as a way of determining the traffic demand, which is an input for all traffic engineering tasks
  • Complete volume study includes classified volume study recording:
    • Volume of different types/classes of traffic
    • Directional distribution
    • Turning movement
    • Distribution on different lane

Objectives or Uses of Traffic Volume Studies

To evaluate and analyze existing facilities, and plan and prioritize improvements

  • Classified volume study for structural design of pavement and geometric design of road
  • Volume distribution study for planning regulatory measures such as one way street
  • Turning movement study for planning and design of intersections and traffic controls
  • Pedestrian volume study for planning and design of pedestrian facilities

Traffic Count Periods

  • Short term (one hour or more)
  • Full day (24 hours)
  • Full week count
  • Continuous count

Methods of Traffic Count

  • Manual Method
  • Automatic Method
  • Video Taping Method
  • Photographic Method
  • Moving Observer (Floating Car) Technique

Manual Method

  • Simplest and basic volume count procedure
  • Observer records the passage of each vehicle/pedestrian in standard sheets according to its classifications, travel direction and turning movements.
  • Tallies are used.
  • Typically used for periods of less than a day
  • Mechanical hand counters
  • Advantages:
    • Classification, directional and turning movement count can be obtained
    • Reliable
    • Less office work
    • Costly and sophisticated equipment are not necessary
  • Disadvantages
    • Subjected to human error
    • Labor intensive
    • Not suitable for long period of count

Automatic Method

  • Automatically record the total number of vehicles crossing a section of the road in a desired period
  • Contact-based system:
    • Involves laying of detector such as pneumatic tube or piezoelectric strips or magnetic loop detector across a road which are connected to a box containing the means for storing the information
  • Contact less system:
    • Ultrasound/infrared radar, micro wave, CCTV/video image processing
  • Ultrasound
  • Infrared
  • Microwave Radar
  • Video Camera
  • TDC1 Traffic Detector
  • Advantages:
    • Suitable for gathering traffic data for longer period
    • Surveyors are not required
  • Disadvantages:
    • Costly & sophisticated equipment are required
    • Need to check accuracy of devices

Video Taping Method

  • Video camera placed at suitable elevated position to record the vehicle movement
  • Recorded video processed at office later
  • Advantages:
    • Less field Surveyors are required
    • Records are permanent and can be reviewed any time later
    • Opportunity to obtain wide range of data
  • Disadvantages:
    • Costly video equipment are required
    • Additional office work is required

Photographic Method

  • Photographs are taken at fixed intervals of time (e.g. 1 sec/ frame) on a special camera.
  • Passage of vehicle is traced with reference to time.

Moving Observer (Floating Car) Technique20822076

  • A test vehicle is driven over a given section at the average speed of the stream

1.2 Traffic Speed and Delay Studies

Past Exam Questions on this Topic

  1. Define spot speed and explain the methods of conducting spot speed study. Write down the advantages and limitations of rotary intersection: [8] (2081 Bhadra, Q2)
    Solution

    Spot speed = instantaneous speed of a vehicle at a specified point on the road.

    Methods of conducting a spot-speed study:

    • Manual: stopwatch + short measured base length, or enoscope (mirror box).
    • Automatic: pneumatic road tubes, inductive loop detectors, Doppler radar gun, LIDAR speed gun, and video / image-processing systems.

    Advantages of a rotary intersection: continuous (stop-free) movement; all crossing manoeuvres converted to merging, weaving and diverging; self-regulating (no signal); eliminates severe right-angle and head-on conflicts; orderly and aesthetic.

    Limitations: requires large land area; unsuitable for very high volumes or heavy pedestrian movement; causes delay under heavy flow; not suited to high-speed traffic or steep gradients; costly where land is expensive.

  2. In a field survey of spot speed, the following twenty observations were taken (in km/h): 50, 40, 60, 54, 45, 31, 72, 18, 43, 52, 46, 56, 43, 65, 33, 69, 34, 51, 47, 41. Find the time mean speed and space mean speed. Also, assuming these vehicle speeds are fixed over a half km segment, calculate the corresponding travel times and show that the space mean speed calculated using travel times is equal to the point estimate. (2075 Chaitra, Q2)
    Solution

    20 spot speeds (km/h): 50, 40, 60, 54, 45, 31, 72, 18, 43, 52, 46, 56, 43, 65, 33, 69, 34, 51, 47, 41. Σv = 950, Σ(1/v) = 0.4643.

    • Time mean speed = Σv/n = 950/20 = 47.5 km/h
    • Space mean speed = n/Σ(1/v) = 20/0.4643 = 43.1 km/h

    Check with travel times over a 0.5 km segment: ti = 0.5/vi. Space mean speed = total distance / total time = (n×0.5)/Σti = (n×0.5)/(0.5×Σ(1/vi)) = n/Σ(1/vi) = 43.1 km/h, i.e. it equals the harmonic-mean (space-mean) point estimate. Note Vs < Vt always.

  4. Assuming a linear speed-density relationship, the mean free speed near zero density is 80 km/h and the corresponding jam density is 130 veh/km. Assume the average length of vehicles is 6 m. i) Write down the speed-density and flow-density equations. ii) Compute the speed and density corresponding to a flow of 1000 veh/hr. iii) Compute the average headways, spacing, clearance and gaps when the flow is maximum. Second part: The following data were collected for a section of road 2.5 km long during a floating-car method study. Assuming equivalency factors of 1, 2 and 3 for car, bus and truck respectively, calculate the directional flow, the journey speed and the running speed of the traffic stream. (The complete floating-car data table - journey time, stopped delay, number of vehicles met from the opposite direction, and overtaking/overtaken counts for each N-S and S-N run - is given in the scanned exam paper on the Past Questions page.) (2072 Chaitra, Q3)
    Solution

    Linear (Greenshields) part: vf = 80 km/h, kj = 130 veh/km.

    v = 80(1 − k/130) = 80 − 0.615k ; q = kv = 80k − 0.615k².

    qmax = vfkj/4 = 80×130/4 = 2600 veh/h at k0 = 65 veh/km, v0 = 40 km/h.

    At q = 1000 veh/h: 0.615k² − 80k + 1000 = 0 → k = 14.0 or 116.0 veh/km, v = q/k = 71.4 km/h (free) or 8.6 km/h (congested).

    At maximum flow: spacing = 1000/k0 = 1000/65 = 15.4 m; headway = 3600/qmax = 3600/2600 = 1.38 s; clearance = spacing − vehicle length = 15.4 − 6 = 9.4 m; gap = clearance / v0 = 9.4/(40/3.6) = 0.85 s.

    Floating-car part: for each direction q = (na + nw)/(ta + tw), journey time t̄ = tw − nw/q, running time = journey time − stopped delay, using PCU factors 1, 2, 3 for car, bus, truck.

    Free speed vf = 80 km/h is taken from the sibling exam papers (the value did not OCR). The floating-car data table is on the scanned Past Questions page; substitute those values into the relations above.

  5. Average trip time for office is 30 minutes with a standard deviation of 5 min. Assuming a normal distribution of trip time, calculate the following: a) Probability of trip time being at least 35 minutes. b) If the working hour starts at 10:00 AM and the trip starts at 9:40 AM, what is the probability of being late? (2071 Chaitra, Q3)
    Solution

    Trip time is normally distributed, μ = 30 min, σ = 5 min.

    (a) P(T ≥ 35): z = (35 − 30)/5 = 1.0 → P = 1 − Φ(1.0) = 1 − 0.8413 = 0.159 (15.9%).

    (b) Probability of being late: available time = 10:00 − 9:40 = 20 min. Late if T > 20 min. z = (20 − 30)/5 = −2.0 → P(T > 20) = P(z > −2) = Φ(2) = 0.977 (97.7%).

Pneumatic Tube Detector

  • Pneumatic tubes laid across the carriageway at the two reference point.
  • When a vehicle passes over them, air impulses are sent to an electromagnetically controlled device.
  • Time elapsed between first impulse and second impulse is recorded as speed.
  • Advantage:
    • Human errors are eliminated
    • Accurate
  • Disadvantage:
    • May affect driver behavior,
    • May disrupt traffic

Inductive Loop Detector

  • A rectangular wire loop buried under the roadway surface, supplied with electromagnetic field
  • A disturbance in the electrical field is created when a motor vehicle passes across it.
  • Time difference between two loop is noted as speed.

  • Instrument is setup near the edge of road at a height of 1m and target the vehicle, which gives speed directly.

Delay

  • Time lost during the travel due to disruption of traffic movement
  • Measured as the time difference between actual travel time and free-flow travel time
  • Types:
    • Fixed: At intersections due to traffic signals
    • Operational: By interference of traffic movements e.g. turning movements and internal friction in the traffic stream e.g. high volume

Speed and Delay Study

  • Determine running speeds, overall speeds, fluctuations in speeds and delay between two stations of a road spaced far apart
  • Uses:
    • To detect spots of congestion and causes
    • To find travel times in specific links
    • To determine level of service
    • For benefit cost analysis
    • For before after studies

License Plate Observations

  • Observers at entrance and exit record license plate number and time of vehicles
  • The license plate number recordings are matched and the difference in time is the travel time of the vehicle
  • Average of travel times of vehicles (at least 50 matched sample) is the average traveling time on the test section

Observation from Vantage Point

  • Average travel time, number of delays, duration and causes are recorded through observations of vehicles from an elevated point
  • Suitable only for short section

Photographic and Video Survey Method

  • Same as in Volume and Speed study
  • Useful for short stretch of section

Interview Method

  • Road users of the study site are interviewed regarding their travel times, experience of delays.
  • Used when large amount of information is needed in short time
  • Requires cooperation of the people

Moving Observer (Floating Car) Technique2072

  • A test vehicle makes a series of runs on a road section on both directions at approximately the average speed of the stream.
  • Minimum 6 runs on each direction

    • Opposing Counter: No. of vehicles travelling in the opposite direction in each trip
    • Tally Counter: No. of vehicles overtaking the test vehicle and no. of vehicles overtaken by the test vehicle in each trip

Basic Traffic Flow Parameters

  • Traffic flow refers to the movement of vehicles through space and time.
  • Traffic flow characteristics are affected by vehicle types, road users, and roadway and traffic conditions.
  • Essential for the analysis, design, and operational management of traffic streams.
  1. Flow (q): Number of vehicles/volume passing a fixed roadside point per unit time (vph)
  2. Speed (v): Space mean speed of vehicles (kmph)
  3. Density (k): Number of vehicles located in a given length of lane or roadway at a particular instant in time (vpkm). The relation between speed, density, and flow is: q = kV (Fundamental relationship in traffic flow theory)

Other Traffic Flow Parameters

  • Spacing/Space Headway (s):
  • Distance between successive vehicles in a traffic stream as measured from front bumper to front bumper.
  • Clearance and Gap:
  • Correspond to spacing (m) and headway (sec) respectively — clearance is the spacing minus the vehicle length, and gap is the headway minus the vehicle's occupancy time.

  1. Define the capacity of a road and list out its types. Two vehicles A and B approaching at right angles, A from west and B from south, collide with each other. After the collision, vehicle A skids in a direction 45° north of west and vehicle B 50° east of north. The initial skid distances of vehicles A and B are 35 m and 25 m respectively before collision. The skid distances after collision are 30 m and 10 m respectively. If the weights of vehicles A and B are 3.5 and 6 tonnes respectively, calculate the original speeds of the vehicles. The average skid resistance of the pavement is found to be 0.55. [2+6] (2080 Bhadra, Q2)
    Solution

    Capacity of a road = the maximum hourly rate at which vehicles can reasonably be expected to traverse a point or uniform section of a lane/roadway under prevailing traffic and roadway conditions. Types: basic (theoretical) capacity, possible capacity, and practical (design) capacity.

    Accident reconstruction. g = 9.81 m/s², f = 0.55.

    Step 1 - speeds just after collision (from post-collision skid, v = √(2 g f d)):

    • Vehicle A (d = 30 m): vA' = √(2×9.81×0.55×30) = 17.99 m/s
    • Vehicle B (d = 10 m): vB' = √(2×9.81×0.55×10) = 10.39 m/s

    Step 2 - momentum conservation at impact (A initially W→E along +x, B initially S→N along +y; after collision A deflects 45° toward N, B 50° E of N). Taking the consistent forward interpretation:

    x: mAuA = mAvA'cos45° + mBvB'sin50° ; y: mBuB = mAvA'sin45° + mBvB'cos50°

    With mA = 3.5 t, mB = 6 t → impact speeds uA ≈ 26.4 m/s, uB ≈ 14.1 m/s.

    Step 3 - original speeds (add energy lost in pre-collision skid, u0 = √(uimpact² + 2 g f dpre)):

    • A (dpre = 35 m): u0A = √(26.4² + 2×9.81×0.55×35) ≈ 32.7 m/s = ≈ 118 km/h
    • B (dpre = 25 m): u0B = √(14.1² + 2×9.81×0.55×25) ≈ 21.6 m/s = ≈ 78 km/h

    Note: the compass directions in the source paper are ambiguous; resolve the momentum components along your own sketch's axes. Method: Marsani - Crash/Accident studies (skid-to-speed + conservation of momentum).

    • u0B = √(16.1² + 2×9.81×0.52×18) = ≈ 21.0 m/s (76 km/h)
  2. A passenger car of mass 430 kg moving east collides with a truck of mass 1299 kg that is moving south. Initial skid distances of the car and the truck are 15 m and 10 m respectively. After collision they lock together and skid through a distance equal to 8 m in a direction 65° south of east before stopping. Compute the initial speeds of the car and the truck. Assume average coefficient of friction as 0.5. [8] (2081 Baishakh, Q2)
    Solution

    Car mc = 430 kg moving East; truck mt = 1299 kg moving South; f = 0.5. After collision they lock together and skid 8 m at 65° S of E.

    Common post-collision speed v' = √(2 g f d) = √(2×9.81×0.5×8) = 8.86 m/s (direction 65° S of E).

    Momentum (perfectly plastic) conservation:

    x(E): mcuc = (mc+mt) v' cos65° → uc = (1729×8.86×0.4226)/430 = 15.05 m/s ≈ 54.2 km/h

    y(S): mtut = (mc+mt) v' sin65° → ut = (1729×8.86×0.9063)/1299 = 10.69 m/s ≈ 38.5 km/h

    (No separate pre-collision skid is given, so these are the speeds at impact.)

  3. Define the capacity of a road and list out its types. Two vehicles A and B approaching at right angles, A from west and B from south, collide with each other. After the collision, vehicle A skids in a direction 45° north of west and vehicle B 50° east of north. The initial skid distances of vehicles A and B are 35 m and 25 m respectively before collision. The skid distances after collision are 30 m and 10 m respectively. If the weights of vehicles A and B are 3.5 and 6 tonnes respectively, calculate the original speeds of the vehicles. The average skid resistance of the pavement is found to be 0.55. [2+6] (2080 Bhadra, Q2)
    Solution

    Capacity of a road = the maximum hourly rate at which vehicles can reasonably be expected to traverse a point or uniform section of a lane/roadway under prevailing traffic and roadway conditions. Types: basic (theoretical) capacity, possible capacity, and practical (design) capacity.

    Accident reconstruction. g = 9.81 m/s², f = 0.55.

    Step 1 - speeds just after collision (from post-collision skid, v = √(2 g f d)):

    • Vehicle A (d = 30 m): vA' = √(2×9.81×0.55×30) = 17.99 m/s
    • Vehicle B (d = 10 m): vB' = √(2×9.81×0.55×10) = 10.39 m/s

    Step 2 - momentum conservation at impact (A initially W→E along +x, B initially S→N along +y; after collision A deflects 45° toward N, B 50° E of N). Taking the consistent forward interpretation:

    x: mAuA = mAvA'cos45° + mBvB'sin50° ; y: mBuB = mAvA'sin45° + mBvB'cos50°

    With mA = 3.5 t, mB = 6 t → impact speeds uA ≈ 26.4 m/s, uB ≈ 14.1 m/s.

    Step 3 - original speeds (add energy lost in pre-collision skid, u0 = √(uimpact² + 2 g f dpre)):

    • A (dpre = 35 m): u0A = √(26.4² + 2×9.81×0.55×35) ≈ 32.7 m/s = ≈ 118 km/h
    • B (dpre = 25 m): u0B = √(14.1² + 2×9.81×0.55×25) ≈ 21.6 m/s = ≈ 78 km/h

    Note: the compass directions in the source paper are ambiguous; resolve the momentum components along your own sketch's axes. Method: Marsani - Crash/Accident studies (skid-to-speed + conservation of momentum).

  4. What are the basic requirements of intersection at grade? Mention the importance of street lighting. Explain the different types of traffic islands. How is accident study carried out? Two vehicles A and B approaching at right angles, vehicle A from West and vehicle B from south, collide with each other. After the collision, vehicle A skids in a direction 49° N of W and vehicle B skids 27° E of N. The initial skid distances of vehicles A and B are 37 m and 19 m respectively before collision. If the weight of vehicle A is 4 tonne and vehicle B is 6 tonne, and the skid distances after collision for vehicle A is 15 m and for vehicle B is 36 m, calculate the initial speeds of the vehicles if the average skid resistance of the pavement is found to be 0.55. (2075 Ashwin, Q1)
    Solution

    Basic requirements of an at-grade intersection: adequate sight distance, minimum conflict points, channelization to guide traffic, adequate capacity, low relative speed, clear and simple layout, provision for pedestrians and proper drainage/lighting.

    Importance of street lighting: improves night visibility & safety, reduces night accidents, aids pedestrian security, improves traffic flow and comfort, and enhances aesthetics.

    Types of traffic islands: divisional islands, channelizing islands, pedestrian refuge islands, and rotary (central) islands.

    Accident study is carried out by collecting accident data (spot maps, collision & condition diagrams), analysing causes (road user, vehicle, road, environment), and applying remedial 3-E measures (Engineering, Enforcement, Education).

    Collision numerical. f = 0.55. Post-collision speeds v = √(2 g f d): vA' = √(2×9.81×0.55×15) = 12.72 m/s; vB' = √(2×9.81×0.55×36) = 19.71 m/s. Apply momentum conservation along E-W and N-S using the given deflection angles (A: 49° N of W, B: 27° E of N) to get the impact speeds, then add the pre-collision skid energy over 37 m and 19 m: u0 = √(uimpact² + 2 g f dpre).

    Weights (A = 4 t, B = 6 t) enter only as the mass ratio in the momentum equations.

  5. What are the causes of accident and how can accidents be prevented? Describe briefly the factors influencing street light design. (2075 Chaitra, Q3)
    Solution

    Causes of accidents (grouped by the 4 elements):

    • Road user: over-speeding, drunk/fatigued driving, disobeying rules, wrong overtaking, carelessness of pedestrians.
    • Vehicle: brake/steering/tyre failure, poor lights, overloading.
    • Road: poor geometry, inadequate sight distance, slippery/poor surface, poor markings.
    • Environment: rain, fog, poor lighting, glare.

    Prevention - the 3 E's: Engineering (better geometry, signs, lighting, road furniture), Enforcement (speed & traffic-law enforcement) and Education (road-safety awareness and training).

  6. A vehicle hits a bridge abutment at a speed estimated by investigations as 20 kmph. Approaching the abutment, the vehicle left skid marks of 60 m on the gravel shoulder (f = 0.50) followed by skid marks of 30 m on the pavement (f = 0.35). What was the initial speed of the vehicle? (2073 Shrawan, Q4)
    Solution

    Skid-to-speed with two surfaces. The vehicle skids 60 m on the gravel shoulder (f = 0.50), then 30 m on the pavement (f = 0.35), and still strikes the abutment at 20 km/h. Work backward from the impact using vbefore² = vafter² + 2 g f d for each phase:

    • Speed at impact = 20 km/h = 5.56 m/s.
    • Start of pavement skid (30 m, f = 0.35): v² = 5.56² + 2×9.81×0.35×30 = 30.9 + 206.0 = 236.9 → v = 15.39 m/s.
    • Start of gravel skid (60 m, f = 0.50): v0² = 15.39² + 2×9.81×0.50×60 = 236.9 + 588.6 = 825.5 → v0 = 28.73 m/s = ≈ 103.4 km/h (initial speed).

    Friction values from the source: gravel shoulder f = 0.50, pavement f = 0.35; impact speed 20 km/h. Method: Marsani - Crash studies (skid-to-speed).

  7. a) Define rotary intersection. What are the advantages and disadvantages of rotary intersection? [8] b) A vehicle skids through a distance of 40m before colliding with another parked vehicle; the weight of which is 75 percent of the former After collision both the vehicles skid through 14m before stopping: Compute the initial speed of moving vehicle. Assume coefficient of friction of 0.62. [8] (2068 Baishakh, Q2)
    Solution

    Rotary intersection = a channelized at-grade intersection with a central island around which all traffic moves one-way (anticlockwise here), converting crossing conflicts into merging, weaving and diverging.

    Advantages: continuous stop-free movement, no severe right-angle/head-on conflicts, self-regulating, orderly, aesthetic. Disadvantages: large land area, poor for high volumes/heavy pedestrian flow, delay under heavy traffic, unsuitable at high speeds or steep grades.

    Numerical. f = 0.62, g = 9.81. A moving vehicle skids 40 m, hits a parked vehicle (weight = 0.75×moving vehicle), then both skid 14 m together.

    • Common speed after impact: v' = √(2 g f d) = √(2×9.81×0.62×14) = 13.06 m/s.
    • Speed of moving vehicle just before impact (plastic collision, m and 0.75m at rest): m·uimpact = (m + 0.75m) v' → uimpact = 1.75×13.06 = 22.86 m/s.
    • Initial speed (add 40 m pre-impact skid): u0 = √(22.86² + 2×9.81×0.62×40) = √(522.6 + 486.6) = 31.77 m/s = ≈ 114 km/h.

Causes of Crash: Road User Factors

  • Perceptual errors (failure to see or understand the traffic situation sign or signal due to lack of attention, distraction or distance)
  • Lack of skill (inexperience, wrong action or decision)
  • Manner of execution (aggressive driving, improper overtaking, carelessness, violation of rules and regulations)
  • Temporary effects (alcohol, fatigue, drugs, sleep, illness, emotional stress)

Causes of Crash: Vehicles

  • Defective tires and brakes, failure of steering system, lighting system etc.
  • Lack of regular maintenance

Causes of Crash: Road and Its Condition

  • Adverse road design (unsuitable layout and junction design)
  • Adverse road surface condition (slippery, lack of maintenance)
  • Inadequate/poor road markings and signs
  • Unexpected obstruction (road works, parked vehicles)

Causes of Crash: Other Environmental Factors

  • Unfavorable weather conditions
  • Animals on the road
  • Advertising boards

Location Files

  • File is maintained by location of the crash
    • A file is maintained for each intersection and midblock.
    • Then filed chronologically for each site.

Spot Maps

  • Each time a crash report is filed, color-coded pin or other symbol is placed on a map at the appropriate location.
    • The color/symbol codes indicate the type and severity of the crash.

Condition Diagram

  • A plan drawn to scale (1:100 to 1:250) showing all the important physical features at the crash location.
    • Aids the process of determining the causes of crash.

Collision Diagram

  • The diagram consists of an outline map of the location and symbols showing the direction of movement of vehicles/pedestrians/objects involved in crashes.
  • Direction of travel.
  • Date and time of accident.
  • Road & weather conditions.
  • Any unusual conditions (flood, storm, etc.)

  3. Analyze summarized facts and field data and suggest remedial measures

  • Road Design and Maintenance: Checking & correcting geometric elements, checking road surfaces and providing maintenance measures, developing solutions to reduce conflict points
  • Vehicle Maintenance: Routine checking and maintenance of braking system, steering, indicators, lighting system and tires

  • Speed control
  • Seat belts and helmets usage
  • Traffic control devices (Heavy fine for violation)
  • Strict testing before issuing license
  • Medical checks (For vision, reaction time, drunk driving)
  • Observance of laws and regulations

Educational Measures

  • Educate road users about the traffic rules
  • Education at school and universities about traffic safety
  • Training course for drivers
  • Traffic safety campaign (Films, documentaries, advertisement etc.)
